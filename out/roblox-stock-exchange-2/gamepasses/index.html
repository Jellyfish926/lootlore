    Roblox Stock Exchange 2 Game Passes: All 13 and Prices

    Scope: Pass names, prices and descriptions from the Roblox game pass API on 2026-10-08; effects beyond the official descriptions are not published; prices can change without notice

    Key points

    • Roblox Stock Exchange 2 sells 13 game passes: 12 single passes from 39 to 299 Robux and the All Gamepasses Bundle at 999.
    • Bought one by one, the 12 single passes add up to 1,778 Robux, so the bundle costs 779 less.
    • Twelve of the descriptions are specific: 0.02% fees, up to 50x leverage at any level, 24 hours of offline market time "instead of 8".
    • Eleven passes also exist as developer products with the same name, and four of those have a different price.

    Roblox Stock Exchange 2 sells 13 game passes. Twelve are single passes priced from 39 Robux (Custom Timeframes) to 299 Robux (Insider), and the thirteenth is the All Gamepasses Bundle at 999 Robux. Bought separately the twelve cost 1,778 Robux. Twelve of the thirteen passes have a specific official description.

    Names, prices and descriptions below come from the Roblox game pass API, read on 8 October 2026. All 13 were on sale and none showed a discount.

    The descriptions are the developer's, copied whole from the Roblox Stock Exchange 2 store. Eight of the passes were created on 13 July 2026, the day the game was created. More Player Companies followed on 14 July, Overnight Desk on 19 July, Custom Timeframes and Unlimited AI Usage on 8 and 9 August, and the bundle on 15 August.

    Is the All Gamepasses Bundle worth it?

    On price alone, yes, if you want most of the passes. The sums are ours:

    OptionRobux
    All 12 single passes bought separately1,778
    All Gamepasses Bundle999
    Difference779 (about 44% less)

    The bundle's description is one line: "All gamepasses, but at a lower cost." It does not name the passes inside it, so we cannot confirm from the listing alone that a pass added later would be included.

    The break-even is simple. If the passes you actually want add up to less than 999 Robux, buy them singly. Insider, VIP, Order Flow, Overnight Desk and Executive Terminal together already come to 1,085.

    Which passes change your trading limits?

    Four passes raise a limit, and for two of them the wording implies what the limit is without the pass.

    PassWith the passWithout it
    Overnight DeskMarket runs 24 hours offline8 hours (implied by "instead of 8")
    More Player CompaniesUp to 5 player-founded stocks2 (implied by "instead of 2")
    Max LeverageUp to 50x leverage at any levelNot stated
    Pro Trader0.02% fees, unlimited drawingsNot stated

    The 8-hour and 2-company figures are implied by the pass descriptions ("instead of 8", "instead of 2"); no source states the base values directly. The fee and the leverage cap for players without a pass are not published anywhere official that we could read. The how-to-play guide explains how these systems fit into a first session.

    Which passes add information or tools?

    Seven more single passes change what you can see or how much of a tool you can use:

    • Order Flow shows "Level 2 order book data" and "bias direction based off the flow of orders".
    • Insider gives "Early alerts before news hits + IPO intel."
    • Indicator Pro lets you "Run up to 8 community or custom indicators at the same time."
    • Watchlist Pro gives "10 custom watchlists with up to 50 symbols in each list."
    • Custom Timeframes unlocks "any custom timeframes, such as 1 second charts and more."
    • Unlimited AI Usage lets you "Ask SummitAI as much as you like", with "no daily or weekly cap".
    • Executive Terminal adds gold branding, "an exclusive set of stocks" and "private market algorithmic bot scans".

    VIP, the twelfth, is the odd one out: "2x XP, 2x daily rewards, VIP badge, 1 free Sim Day per day." If a "Sim Day" is the same thing as the Simulate Day product (39 Robux), that perk would be worth that much; the listings do not say so. What the bot scans do is covered in the algo bot guide.

    Why do four passes have a different product price?

    The store lists eleven of the passes twice, and in four cases the two prices differ. The listings do not say why. Eleven of the 13 passes have a developer product with exactly the same name:

    The listings do not say what the products are for, and we will not guess. The practical point: read the price on the Roblox purchase prompt before you confirm. If it shows 399 for VIP, you are looking at the product, and the pass itself costs 229. The rest of the store is in the Robux shop guide.

    Do passes run out?

    No. Roblox defines a pass as "a one-time Robux fee to access special privileges inside your game". That is the difference from the shop's time skips and AI Tokens, which are developer products. Roblox defines a developer product as "an item or ability that a user can purchase more than once".

    Which pass should you buy first?

    That depends on what frustrates you, and these are our suggestions, not the developer's:

    • You play in short sessions. Overnight Desk is described as 24 hours of offline market time "instead of 8".
    • You trade often. Pro Trader names a fee rate (0.02%). The fee without the pass is not published, so we cannot say how much it changes.
    • You want to see more. Custom Timeframes (39) and Watchlist Pro (49) are the two cheapest passes.
    • You want bigger positions. Max Leverage raises the cap. Leverage multiplies losses as well as gains, even with simulated money.

    Before spending, check the codes page for the code printed in the description.
