    Beast of Reincarnation: How Long to Beat

    Post-launch data · Updated August 31, 2026

    Language: English日本語FrançaisEspañolItalianoDeutsch

    Length

    Measured post-launch: a focused run reaches the credits in about 20 hours, an average playthrough lands at 25–28, and completionists report 30–35 — across 13 chapters plus an epilogue. The full chapter and region list is below.

    Quick answer: post-launch reporting puts a fast, focused playthrough at about 20 hours, a typical run at 25–28 hours, and a completionist route at 30–35 hours (VGC's measured chapter-list guide). That runs a little under the director's pre-launch “about 30 hours” figure. Note that 100% of the achievement list additionally requires New Game Plus — see the trophy roadmap.

    How long is Beast of Reincarnation?

    The measured post-launch picture, playstyle by playstyle:

    PlaystyleLengthBasis
    Fast, focused main story~20 hrsVGC post-launch guide (measured chapter route)
    Average playthrough25–28 hrsVGC post-launch guide
    Completionist single run30–35 hrsVGC post-launch guide; exploration and leveling extend this
    True 100% (all achievements)Two runs — NG+ requiredSteam list: “Reincarnated” (NG+) at 3.0%, capstone “Duty Fulfilled” at 2.7%

    Pre-launch, director Kota Furushima had estimated “about 30 hours,” and IGN's preview guessed 30–40 — the shipped game runs a touch faster than both for the main path. Where the sources disagree, we show the measured post-launch figures first and keep the older estimates for context.

    Those numbers put Beast of Reincarnation in the same scope bracket as Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice, Stellar Blade, and Final Fantasy XVI — a substantial but focused action RPG, not a 100-hour open-world epic.

    Every chapter and region

    The campaign runs 13 chapters plus an epilogue: Seedling, Creeping Tendrils, The Vessel, Stems, Branching Paths, Necrosis, Root Rot, Worm Food, Branching Out, Malefact, Budding Realization, The Firmament, To Bloom Then Fall, and the epilogue Bearing Fruit.

    Those chapters cross five broad areas of Blighted Japan, subdivided into twelve-plus zones: the Kanto Region (Ogouchi Buffer Zone, Ancient Civilization Zone), the Mountainous Region (Great Wall Terraces, Pilgrim's Zone, Matsukawa Zone, Fuji Frontier Zone), the Drowned Region (Giant Tree Zone), the Wasteland Region (Crater Zone, Inukami River Zone, Starfall Tower), and the Capital Zone (Mount Hiei Zone, The Capital, Center of the Blight).

    Steam's global data adds the reality check: 53% of players seal the first boss and about 24% finish Chapter 13 — the funnel is steepest in the first two chapters, so budget your retry patience early. Boss-by-boss levels and chapters are on the boss list.

    Where the numbers come from

    The headline figures are from VGC's post-launch chapter-list guide, which measured a fast route at 20 hours, an average at 25–28, and completionist play at 30–35 — and which notes that skipping exploration and leveling makes late bosses harder, stretching the “fast” route right back out. The completion funnel figures are Steam's official global achievement statistics.

    The same previews clarified the game's shape. Beast of Reincarnation is largely linear: there is less side content than in a typical open-world RPG, but each stage contains open areas with secrets and off-the-beaten-path routes worth exploring. Press reaction to the preview builds has been cautiously optimistic, with IGN noting that it is the combination of traversal, RPG systems, and hard-action combat — rather than any single element — that makes the game stand out among souls-like action games.

    The structure behind the runtime

    Beast of Reincarnation is not open world. Emma and her canine partner Koo travel east to west across a post-apocalyptic Japan consumed by the Blight, a corruption that wiped out most of humanity. Each of the 10 stages is a wide "district level" anchored by a Nushi — a giant corrupted boss lurking in that region's Blighted Forest. Sealing a Nushi lets Emma absorb its power, which is the core progression hook and the reason the campaign has such a clear, measurable rhythm: explore a district, find its Nushi, win, grow stronger, move west.

    Between bosses, the growth systems give the clock its texture. Emma advances through a skill tree, equipment upgrades, and Spirit Stones, while Koo contributes support skills selected from a menu that slows time — a deliberate contrast to Emma's real-time, parry-heavy swordplay. None of these systems demand grinding by design, but players who like maxing out a build before a boss will naturally spend longer in each district.

    What will change your personal time

    • Parry skill. Combat is built around deflection and clashing blades, in the SEKIRO tradition. If that rhythm clicks for you, bosses fall in a handful of attempts; if it doesn't, retries add up fast. The beginner guide covers the fundamentals.
    • Build path. Game Freak has confirmed three viable approaches — ranged, stealth, and all-out aggression. Stealth runs tend to be slower per encounter but safer; aggressive melee is faster when it works and costlier when it doesn't. See our builds page for details.
    • Exploration appetite. District levels are wide, and previews confirm secrets and optional routes in each one. Sweeping every corner for gear and Spirit Stones is the difference between the low and high ends of the estimate.
    • Difficulty settings. Three modes ship in the game, including a Story Mode; a lower setting mostly compresses boss-retry time, which is where the hours hide in this genre.

    Platforms, price, and the Game Pass factor

    Where you play doesn't change the length, but it changes the cost of finding out. There is still no demo, so Game Pass remains the cheapest way to sample the opening chapters before committing.

    The Digital Deluxe Edition adds Koo skins (black and brown Shiba), Emma's Oni hat, the sword Big Dipper, 100,000 Amber, and crop seedlings; pre-ordering any edition grants the brown Shiba Koo skin plus 30,000 Amber. These are cosmetics and starter boosts — none of them meaningfully shortens or lengthens the campaign.

    FAQ

    Is Beast of Reincarnation open world?

    No. It is a largely linear, stage-based game — 13 chapters across five areas. The districts are wide and hide optional paths, but there is no seamless open map.

    How long does 100% completion take?

    Not yet confirmed. No official trophy or achievement list has been published, so nobody can credibly quote a completionist figure yet. Given the ~30-hr main story and secrets in every stage, expect it to sit above the standard-run estimate.

    Is there a demo I can try first?

    No demo has been announced as of late August 2026. On Xbox and PC, Game Pass Ultimate includes the full game on day one, which effectively serves that purpose.

    Does the Game Pass version differ from the paid version?

    No. It is the same game, and as an Xbox Play Anywhere title your saves and achievements sync between Xbox Series X|S and PC.

    Is there a New Game Plus mode?

    Yes. The Steam achievement list includes “Reincarnated” for starting New Game Plus (3.0% of players), so a second run is required for the 100% capstone — Game Freak has not published a breakdown of what carries over.
