Beginner Guide: Your First Hours in the Live Game

Beast of Reincarnation is out now, and it plays exactly as billed: a parry-first souls-like where your defense literally powers your offense. This guide has been rewritten from the shipping game to cover the systems you actually touch in the opening hours — deflection, FP and Koo's Bloom Arts, the Down Gauge, Entanglement Overdrive — plus how to spend your first skill points without regret.

1. Combat 101: the whole game is the parry

Emma fights with a katana, a bow, and a crossbow, but the beating heart of the system is deflection. A perfect block beats a dodge almost every time: the game is built in the cadence of Sekiro, where meeting an attack head-on with clean timing is the intended answer to most enemy strings. Dodging exists and has its place, but if you try to play Beast of Reincarnation as a roll-and-punish game you will fight everything with half your kit switched off. Internalize this before anything else: you defend in order to attack.

Treat every basic Malefact — the corrupted creatures that infest the world — in the opening hours as a free parry lesson. The timing you build against trash mobs is the exact timing you'll spend against every boss, so there is no such thing as wasted practice early on.

2. FP and Koo's Bloom Arts — defense feeds offense

Here is why parrying is non-negotiable rather than just stylish. Every clean deflection banks FP (Florescence Points), and FP is the fuel for Koo's Bloom Arts — the powerful commands you give your canine partner. Skip parries and the pool stays empty; keep it empty and Koo's strongest tools never fire. This is the loop the whole game is built around: parry to earn FP, spend FP on Bloom Arts, repeat.

Bloom Arts do the heavy lifting a lone swordfighter can't. Captures from the live game show them breaking enemy guard, pinning a target in place, hitting groups with acid-style area damage and status ailments, closing distance, and even healing Emma. (Treat those as examples, not an official complete list — the exact Bloom Art names and their precise FP costs are still being verified post-launch, so confirm them in your own menu.) The beginner takeaway is simple: FP is not a resource to hoard. Points you die holding are wasted, and the pool refills every time you parry, so spend aggressively.

3. Fill the Down Gauge to open enemies up

Landing hits and successful parries build a Down Gauge. Fill it and you can execute a weaker Malefact outright, or stagger a boss to open a window for heavy damage. This is the offensive payoff of playing the deflection game well: aggressive, accurate parrying doesn't just keep you alive, it drives enemies toward the staggered state where fights are actually won. If a boss feels spongy, you're almost certainly under-using the Down Gauge.

4. Entanglement Overdrive: your panic-and-punish button

Emma has a separate Entanglement Gauge that fills across seven layers. Cash it in for Entanglement Overdrive, a slow-time state that lets you land a flurry while the world crawls. It can be upgraded through five levels and is tied to the Strength attribute, so investing in Strength makes your Overdrive hit harder. Save it for boss phase transitions or the moment a fight tips against you — it is both an emergency exit and a burst-damage window.

5. Koo and the command menu

Koo is Emma's withered, wolf-like companion, and he is semi-controlled rather than fully automatic. On PS5 you press Triangle (△) to open his command menu (the exact button on Xbox and PC is still being verified post-launch). Opening it slows combat dramatically but does not pause it — Emma still has to move and parry in real time while you choose. Beginners should lean on this: overwhelmed by a swarm, or blindsided by a new boss phase, open the menu, read the arena, then commit. It is not a crutch, it is the design — the bridge between Game Freak's methodical heritage and real-time action.

Koo is more than a weapon. Petting and interacting with him builds Rapport, which raises his combat power and unlocks rewards, so the affection system is mechanically real. Out in the world he barks to warn of ambushes, points you toward hidden loot and rest points, digs up items, and can pull enemy aggro off you. A few details — his exact PC/Xbox binds, the full Bloom Arts roster, and what happens if he goes down — are still being verified. See the Koo guide for the current picture.

6. Weapons and the three attributes

Loadouts are simpler than the genre usually is, which makes early decisions easier. There are exactly three weapon types:

  • Katana — your melee weapon. It can be swapped for others and carries elemental properties.
  • Bow and Crossbow — your ranged options. Arrows and bolts can be socketed with up to three element slots.

The elements are Fire (ignites, then explodes), Lightning (applies Shock), and Wet. Learning which enemies fold to which element is a real part of the game — the very first boss, for one, is weak to Fire.

Character growth runs on just three attributes, so there is no sprawling stat screen to agonize over:

  • Strength — raises both attack and defense (and powers Entanglement Overdrive).
  • Vitality — raises HP.
  • Florescence — raises HP and skill power.

From these, four broad playstyles emerge: melee pressure, ranged elemental, stealth execution, and a Koo-focused parry build leaning on Bloom Arts. All four are viable — the builds guide breaks down where they diverge. The single best early habit is to pick a direction and concentrate your skill points around the gear effects that support it, rather than spreading thin.

7. The Cleansing Walker and how you get stronger

Your hub is the Cleansing Walker — the base where you save and upgrade between forays. Progression there runs on three parallel tracks worth understanding from hour one:

  • SP levels up a pair of skill trees — Emma has her own line, and Koo grows on a separate one.
  • Spirit Stones slot into your armor's sockets for passive bonuses — the closest thing here to a gem or rune system.
  • Nushi Skills are the marquee unlocks: beating a main boss grants Emma a new signature ability absorbed from it, which is why boss order and preparation matter more than in a typical souls-like.

Builds can be respecced, so early skill-point mistakes aren't permanent — experiment freely. And note that full completion requires New Game Plus, so your first run is not your last.

8. Your very first boss: Rangifer

The opening major fight is Rangifer, a giant deer-like Malefact nicknamed "The Prowling Nushi," which you'll face around level 6–7. Two concrete tips straight from the live game. First, it is weak to Fire, so bring a fire-element loadout and lean on one of Koo's fire Bloom Arts to ignite it (the exact art's name varies by source — confirm it in your own menu). Second, its telegraphed horizontal antler sweep is your FP piggy bank: parry it cleanly and repeatedly to bank the points that fund Koo. You'll fight Rangifer more than once, so the pattern you drill here pays off immediately. Full strategies for the later bosses are still being verified post-launch — see the boss hub for what's confirmed so far.

9. First-hours priorities

  1. Parry first, everything else second. Spend the opening area drilling deflection timing on trash mobs. It is the currency you'll spend for the rest of the game.
  2. Keep spending FP on Koo. The pool refills through parries. A full FP bar at the moment you die is pure waste.
  3. Pick a direction and commit skill points to synergy. Concentrate SP around the gear effects that reinforce your chosen playstyle instead of dabbling in all four.
  4. Learn the elements early. Fire, Lightning (Shock), and Wet each have their moments — Rangifer alone will teach you why Fire matters.
  5. Take care of Koo. Petting him raises Rapport and, with it, his combat power. It is not just flavor.
  6. Use the Cleansing Walker between forays. Save, spend SP, socket Spirit Stones, and reassess before pushing into the next region.

10. Mistakes to avoid

  • Hoarding FP. The pool is designed to be spent and refilled. Sitting on it means fighting with half your kit dark.
  • Playing it like a roll-heavy souls-like. Deflection, not dodging, is the intended defense. Rolling everything leaves the Down Gauge — and your best damage — on the table.
  • Living in (or forgetting) the command menu. It slows time but doesn't pause it. Use it as punctuation between deflection phrases, not a place to hide.
  • Spreading skill points thin. Four playstyles are viable, but a focused build clears walls a scattered one stalls on. You can respec later, so commit now.
  • Buying on Xbox or PC without checking Game Pass first. If you already pay for Game Pass or PC Game Pass, the game is included day one. PS5 players have no such option and should compare the editions instead.
  • Expecting a second, "true" ending from a checklist. The game has a single main-story ending followed by New Game Plus. Don't chase mechanics that aren't there.

A note on performance

Two things worth knowing before you install. On PC the game requires an SSD, wants 16GB of RAM as a floor, supports DLSS/FSR upscaling, and takes roughly 45GB. On PS5, Performance mode targets 60fps but drops frames in boss fights and ships with some low-resolution textures. Steam Deck was not Verified at launch — if you play there, locking to 40fps on Low settings is the pragmatic starting point. Boss-fight camera and PC/Steam Deck performance are the two most common complaints in launch reviews, so temper expectations accordingly. The performance guide and PC requirements pages go deeper.

Beginner FAQ

Is the game out, and what's the cheapest way to try it?

Yes — it launched August 4, 2026. On Xbox and PC it's included day one with Xbox Game Pass or PC Game Pass, which is the cheapest way to try it. PS5 has no subscription option, so there it's a straight purchase.

Do I need to have played Sekiro or other souls-likes first?

No. The Sekiro comparison is about the parry-centric rhythm, not a barrier to entry. What matters is committing to deflection over dodging and keeping FP flowing to Koo; those two habits carry you further than genre experience.

What's the difference between the standard and Deluxe editions?

The $69.99 Digital Deluxe adds black and brown skins for Koo, Emma's "Oni's Hat," a sword called "Big Dipper," 100,000 Amber, and crop seedlings on top of the $59.99 standard game. Every one of those is a cosmetic, an item, or currency — the $10 gap does not affect the core gameplay in any way.

How long is the game, and is there more after the credits?

Roughly 30 hours for the main story, about 40 with side content, and around 80 for full completion (these figures are approximate). There is a single main-story ending followed by New Game Plus, which you'll need for full achievements — so the first run isn't the end.

Can I fix a bad build?

Yes. Builds can be respecced and the level cap is 100, so early skill-point choices aren't locked in. Experiment with the four playstyles freely.

Should I play with a controller or keyboard on PC?

Both are supported on Steam and the Microsoft Store. For a deflection-timing game a controller is the safer default, but either works.
