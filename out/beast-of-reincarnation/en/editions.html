    Beast of Reincarnation Editions & Pre-Order Bonuses

    Edition contents finalized as storefronts publish them

    Language: English日本語FrançaisEspañolItalianoDeutsch

    Buying guide

    Standard, Digital Deluxe, or Collectors Edition? Here's every version, the confirmed $59.99 base price, pre-order bonuses, and the one thing Game Pass players should know before paying.

    Quick take: Beast of Reincarnation launches August 4, 2026 at $59.99 / 59,99 € / ¥8,980 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC (Steam and Microsoft Store). Every edition contains the same full game — the paid extras are cosmetics, in-game currency, and one bonus weapon. And if you already pay for Game Pass Ultimate, you can play on day one without buying anything at all.

    Editions at a glance

    EditionPriceWhat you get
    Standard (digital)$59.99 / 59,99 € / ¥8,980The full game on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, or PC via Steam or the Microsoft Store.
    Digital DeluxeHigher than standard; exact price varies by storefrontThe full game plus two alternate Shiba skins for Koo (black and brown), the Oni's Hat for Emma, the Big Dipper sword, 100,000 Amber, and a set of vegetable seedlings.
    Physical (PS5 / Xbox Series X)Standard pricing; check your retailerA boxed copy of the game, available to pre-order at major North American retailers. The Japanese PS5 physical release, published by Happinet, also seals in a code for the Deluxe-equivalent DLC.
    Pre-order bonus (any edition)Free with pre-orderBrown Shiba skin for Koo plus 30,000 Amber.

    That's the entire lineup as of late August 2026. Fictions, Inc. — the publisher handling Game Freak's first original AAA action RPG — has kept the structure unusually simple for a modern release: one base price, one deluxe tier, and a physical run. No gameplay, story chapters, or difficulty options are locked behind the more expensive version.

    What's inside the Digital Deluxe Edition

    The Digital Deluxe bundle for Beast of Reincarnation is built entirely from cosmetics and early-economy items:

    • Black Shiba and Brown Shiba skins for Koo. Alternate coats for Emma's canine partner. Purely visual — Koo fights, sniffs out paths, and tanks Nushi attention the same way in every outfit.
    • Oni's Hat. A demon-horned headpiece for Emma. Cosmetic headgear that leans into the game's post-apocalyptic-Japan folklore aesthetic.
    • Big Dipper. A bonus sword for Emma. Whether its stats differ meaningfully from early-game weapons is not yet confirmed, so treat it as a style option rather than a power spike for now.
    • 100,000 Amber. A large stipend of the in-game currency, useful for early upgrades and supplies.
    • Vegetable seedlings. Starter crops that feed into the game's cultivation system.

    None of this changes the core loop. Beast of Reincarnation is a deflection-first action RPG in the SEKIRO mold — clash blades, break posture, punish — and no amount of Amber buys you parry timing. Progression runs through a skill tree, equipment, and Spirit Stones, and all three confirmed playstyles (ranged, stealth, and all-out aggression) are available in every edition. The 100,000 Amber stipend is a convenience for the opening hours — it covers early upgrades and consumables — but Amber is earned steadily through normal play, so it does not change the shape of the campaign.

    Pre-order bonuses and how they stack

    Pre-ordering any version of Beast of Reincarnation adds the Brown Shiba skin for Koo and 30,000 Amber. These stack with the Deluxe contents, so a Digital Deluxe pre-order starts you with both Shiba coats, the hat, the sword, the seedlings, and a combined 130,000 Amber.

    One thing worth knowing before you commit: there is no demo as of late August 2026. Game Freak showed extended gameplay at Xbox's Developer Direct and in later trailers, and press previews have been cautiously optimistic about the Sekiro-style combat, but nobody outside the studio has played the full game yet. If you're on the fence, nothing about the pre-order bonus is significant enough to justify buying before reviews land — a dog skin and a modest Amber head start are nice-to-haves, not must-haves.

    Game Pass, Play Anywhere, and whether you need to buy at all

    Beast of Reincarnation is a day-one Game Pass Ultimate title. It's also an Xbox Play Anywhere game, which means the Xbox and PC versions share one license, one save file, and one achievement list — start a boss run on Series X, finish it on your desktop.

    For Xbox and PC players, that reframes the whole buying decision. If you subscribe to Game Pass Ultimate anyway, playing through the subscription is effectively free, and the only reason to spend money is if you specifically want the Deluxe cosmetics and Amber. Whether those Deluxe items will be sold separately as an add-on for Game Pass players is not yet confirmed by the storefronts.

    PS5 players have no equivalent option — there is no Game Pass on PlayStation, so the choice there is simply Standard versus Digital Deluxe versus the physical disc.

    The Japanese physical edition is quietly the best deal

    In Japan and parts of Asia, Happinet publishes the boxed PS5 release, and it comes with a notable perk: a sealed-in serial code granting the same bonus DLC as the Digital Deluxe Edition. That's a permanent inclusion in the package, not a launch-window extra, which makes the Japanese physical version the cheapest way to get the full Deluxe item set — if importing is practical for you.

    Japanese retailers are also layering on shop-exclusive physical goods such as canvas art boards and acrylic stands. Those extras vary store by store and are aimed squarely at collectors; check the official Japanese site for the current list before choosing where to order.

    Which edition should you buy?

    • Game Pass Ultimate subscriber? Buy nothing. Play day one on Xbox or PC through the subscription, with Play Anywhere syncing saves between the two. Only spend money if the cosmetics matter to you.
    • PS5 player? Standard at $59.99 is the sensible default. Go Deluxe only if you like Koo's alternate coats and want the Amber cushion — the game itself is identical.
    • Souls-like veteran? Skip the Deluxe. A posture-and-parry game lives or dies on your reactions, and the bonus gear won't blunt the challenge or trivialize the Nushi fights.
    • Collector or importer? The Japanese PS5 physical edition with its sealed-in Deluxe code is the value pick, and shop-exclusive goods sweeten it further.

    Money-saver: the cheapest path to playing Beast of Reincarnation at launch is Game Pass Ultimate on Xbox or PC. The best physical value is the Japanese PS5 release with its bundled Deluxe-equivalent DLC code.

    Editions FAQ

    Is there a Beast of Reincarnation demo?

    No. As of late August 2026 no demo has been announced for any platform. Trailers and the Xbox Developer Direct segment are the only public footage.

    Does the Digital Deluxe Edition make the game easier?

    Not meaningfully. The skins and hat are cosmetic, and while 100,000 Amber helps the early economy, the combat is skill-based deflection in the SEKIRO tradition — money doesn't parry for you.

    How much does Beast of Reincarnation cost?

    The standard edition is $59.99 in the US, 59,99 € in Europe, and ¥8,980 in Japan — notably under the $69.99 price point many AAA releases now carry.

    Can I get the Deluxe items if I play on Game Pass?

    Not yet confirmed. Storefronts haven't announced whether the Deluxe content will be sold as a standalone add-on for subscribers.

    Is there a Collectors Edition with physical goodies?

    The physical PS5 and Xbox Series X releases plus Japanese shop-exclusive items (canvas boards, acrylic stands) are the collector options announced so far. No premium statue-and-artbook box has been announced as of late August 2026.
