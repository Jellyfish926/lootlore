<h2>Buying it</h2>
<p><strong>What does it cost?</strong> $29.99 for the standard edition. The Deluxe bundle is $44.98 — base game plus the $19.99 Deluxe Pack at a bundle discount. (A June roadmap stream was reported with Deluxe at $49.99; the Steam price today is what counts.)</p>
<p><strong>What is the DLC situation?</strong> Around twenty items, all cosmetic: costume sets at $3.99–$7.99 and <a href="/guides/familiars/">Familiars</a> — the rideable companions — at $3.99, with one Familiar free. Nothing sold affects power. New sets have shipped steadily since launch ("Summer Sea Set" on 31 July, "Moonlight on the Night Sea" on 6 August), and a free limited-time Familiar, the Abyssal Direwolf, was given away in late July.</p>
<p><strong>Is it selling well enough to be supported?</strong> The developer's thank-you post around 11 August announced <strong>300,000 copies sold and a million wishlists</strong>. Reviews sit at Very Positive — roughly 90% of about 9,800 — and concurrent players were around 13–14 thousand in mid-August against a 23,030 launch peak. None of that guarantees anything, but a self-published studio with those numbers has every reason to keep shipping its roadmap.</p>
<h2>The gacha question</h2>
<p><strong>Is DragonSword: Awakening a gacha game?</strong> No — and the reason people ask is that <strong>it used to be one.</strong> This game began life as Project D, a free-to-play gacha mobile title whose Korean service launched in January 2026 under the publisher Webzen. Hound13 terminated that agreement — reporting says over roughly 60% of contractual minimum payments going unpaid; the CEO's line was that the studio and a live-service game could not both survive with payments frozen — and rebuilt the game as a buy-to-play Steam release with the gacha stripped out.</p>
<p>Webzen sought an injunction to block the Steam launch. The court rejected its claims and the game shipped; the broader lawsuit continues.</p>
<p>What that history means in your library today: all 19 heroes are earned by playing — story unlocks plus <strong>Fate's Invitation</strong> tickets you spend on the hero of your choice, no randomness — and money buys only the cosmetics listed above. The four roadmap heroes are explicitly free, not DLC.</p>
<h2>Platforms and hardware</h2>
<p><strong>Is it on PS5 or Switch?</strong> Not yet. As of early August 2026, Hound13 says <strong>PlayStation and Nintendo versions have started development</strong> — no platforms confirmed beyond that, no dates. Today it is Windows-only on Steam; no Mac, no Linux.</p>
<p><strong>How is it on Steam Deck?</strong> Better than a UE5 open world has any right to be, per Steam Deck HQ's testing: roughly 30–40 FPS at medium settings with FSR on Balanced, and a stable 30 with effects lowered and TSR or FSR at higher quality, dipping only in the largest fights. We could not confirm an official Verified/Playable badge, so treat it as playable-in-practice rather than certified.</p>
<p><strong>Controller?</strong> DualShock and DualSense are supported; keyboard-and-mouse is listed as partial support, and controller improvements are on the roadmap. One hard requirement from the store page: <strong>an SSD is mandatory</strong>, with a 25 GB install.</p>
<p><strong>Can I play offline?</strong> Unverified — the store page does not say, and no guide we trust has tested it. Co-op is peer-to-peer with no dedicated servers, which implies nothing either way about the single-player mode. Marked open rather than guessed.</p>
<h2>What is coming</h2>
<p>From the developer's Stream #4 roadmap (19 June 2026): <strong>four free heroes</strong> arriving through late 2026 — Ryza (shock, teleporting spear), then Jerome (knockdown, greatsword and grabs), Veronica (stun, close-quarters fists and guns), and Logan (a shield tank) — plus new Hero Quests starting with Othello's, the <strong>Rift</strong> system adding dimensional fissures across the map, and quality-of-life work on combat feel, controllers, sprinting and the camera. Paid side: five costume packs and two swimsuit sets, cosmetic as ever.</p>
<p><strong>Latest patch?</strong> Still <strong>1.0.8</strong> (6 August) as of this page's date — two bug fixes, nothing balance-related. The launch fortnight averaged a patch every day or two, so the slowdown reads as stabilisation, not abandonment.</p>
