3:T1b41,<p>Sephiria's inventory is a build because every cell carries a number, and tablets are what put numbers on cells. The <a href="/tablets/all-tablets/">tablet families</a> page explains the four kinds of tablet and the <a href="/tablets/tablet-solver-and-placement/">solver page</a> explains the tool that optimises them. This page is the step between: five layouts that documented builds actually use, worked cell by cell, so the abstract rules — reach 3, cap +5, line buffs, edge anchors, parity — turn into something you can reproduce on your own grid tonight.</p>
<p>All grids below assume the launch <strong>6-wide</strong> rows; the starter bag is 6×4 and the Destiny Inscription's Expanding Pouch adds one full row.</p>

<h2>Layout 1 — the free row: Endure 5's Foundation</h2>
<p><strong>Foundation</strong> is a line tablet: +1 to its entire row. The <a href="/guides/talents/">Endure talent</a> at level 5 hands you one at the start of every run, before you have found anything, which is why three separate build routes call Endure 5 "nearly free value."</p>
<p>The layout is trivial but the <em>decision</em> is not: put Foundation in the row where your <strong>max-level-1 and max-level-2 artifacts</strong> will live. A +1 row lifts every level-0 item in it by one, and for an item whose maximum is 1 that is a maxed item for free. Do not spend the row on artifacts that cap at 4 or 5 — they want the layouts below.</p>
<h2>Layout 2 — the tallest cell: Requital under Conviction</h2>
<p><strong>Requital</strong> is the strongest single line effect (+3 to its whole row — +18 of distributed value on a 6-wide grid). <strong>Conviction</strong> holds the largest single-cell buff in the game, +5. Stack them:</p>
<p>``<code> Row A:  [ . ][ R ][ . ][ X ][ . ][ . ]   ← Requital's row: every cell +3; X also receives Conviction's +5 Row B:  [ . ][ . ][ . ][ C ][ . ][ . ]   ← Conviction, placed so its target cell is X </code>``</p>
<p>If Conviction's target cell sits in Requital's row, that cell reads <strong>+8</strong> — enough to max almost anything, since max level varies per artifact and few go above 5. Everything else in the row still gets +3. This is the "line first, then discrete on top" rule from the tablet families page in one picture, and it is how the <a href="/builds/build-routes/">Ilonica staff build</a> holds its four core items at exact levels of 3 / 4 / 5 / 5.</p>
<p>Check the target with the in-game <strong>Q / E</strong> preview before you commit: Conviction's offset is fixed relative to its own cell, and a +8 on a max-3 artifact wastes five levels.</p>
<h2>Layout 3 — the reserved top row: Multipurpose Belt</h2>
<p>The <strong>Multipurpose Belt</strong> grants +1 to all three elemental stats <em>per artifact in the top row</em>, up to +6 each. The Ilonica staff build's author — the same person who maintains the solver — reserves the entire top row for it and warns that "not maxing [the core items] hurts the build drastically."</p>
<p>This is a layout driven by an <em>artifact</em> rule rather than a tablet rule, and it changes where your tablets go: line tablets that would normally sit on the top row move down one, and a top-row <strong>Shade</strong> (an edge-anchored tablet that only works on the top edge) is suddenly competing with six artifact slots. The solver's Items Update added exactly these grid-dependent artifacts to its Rulesets so you can ask for "top row full" and let it place the rest.</p>
<h2>Layout 4 — the damage line: compasses under a spellbook</h2>
<p>The <a href="/builds/lightning-boomerang-spellbook-build/">lightning boomerang</a> guide's core trick is positional: place <strong>compasses in a contiguous line directly under the boomerang spellbook</strong>, "connected with nothing in between," each adding +35% by the author's count — six in a row is +210%. Any tablet that breaks the line breaks the bonus.</p>
<p>So the tablets on that build go <em>around</em> the compass row, not through it: Requital on a different row, Foundation where the low-cap support items live, and nothing at all on the cells between compasses. It is the clearest case of a build where the <a href="/guides/combo-effects/">combo tags</a> and the grid geometry are the same decision.</p>
<h2>Layout 5 — the whole-board flip: Curse</h2>
<p><strong>Curse</strong>, from the Skeleton costume, is the only parity tablet: odd cells +1, even cells −1 across the <em>entire</em> grid, regardless of where it sits — and moving it one cell in any direction inverts which colour is buffed. Rotation does nothing.</p>
<p>The worked use: put your maxable artifacts on one colour and your cannot-be-helped items (already maxed, or level-0 filler) on the other, then place Curse so the buffed colour is the one that matters. Two Curses on same-parity cells stack to +2 / −2; on opposite parity they cancel exactly, which is a real way to switch the effect off without discarding a tablet. The solver models it (added at a Skeleton player's request), so you can test both parities in a second rather than re-placing in-game.</p>
<h2>The two mistakes that waste a grid</h2>
<p><strong>Edge tablets off their edge.</strong> Good Will (bottom), Shade (top), Flag (left) and Justice (left or right) do nothing anywhere else while still eating a cell. Place them first or leave them on the floor.</p>
<p><strong>Reach.</strong> Maximum discrete reach is <strong>3 cells</strong> (Manhattan distance). A tablet in a corner aiming at the far side of a 6-wide row is aiming at nothing — which is why the solver never proposes it.</p>
<h2>Why a whole extra row is worth more than any tablet</h2>
<p>The tier 50 guide's rule was "take bag slots — every successful run I've had at higher difficulties had me getting an entire row." Every layout above gets easier with one more row: Requital's +3 covers six more cells, the Belt's reserved row stops competing with your line tablets, and a compass line has somewhere to go. Slots come from the <a href="/guides/destiny-inscription/">Destiny Inscription</a> (+6, one full row), Ingenuity 5 (+2) and Survival 10 (+3); Survival 20's Tablet Imprinting frees cells by consuming tablets, and since 1.0.30 a failed imprint no longer eats the tablet — but it costs 12 slots up front, which is two of these layouts gone.
