    Valheim Farming: Seeds and Steady Food

    Author Jellyfi · Published 2026-09-17 · Last checked 2026-09-17 · Checked on version 1.0.12

    Scope: Vanilla standard survival; world modifiers, mods and later patches can change the rules

    Language: English中文

    Cultivator

    Quick facts

    Requires
    Crafted at a Forge
    Unlocked by
    Obtaining Bronze and Corewood
    Material

    Key points

    • Do not eat the first harvest — split the field's output into seed stock, current cooking and a reserve.

    • Crops have biome requirements: barley needs the Plains, so a Meadows base cannot carry your farming through every stage.
    • Unhealthy crops get checked in order — in-game prompt, biome, spacing, structures overhead, nearby obstacles — not ripped up and replanted.

    Do not eat your first harvest. Farming really begins not when you harvest once, but when every harvest still leaves you able to plant the next round. Split what the field produces three ways: seed stock, current cooking, and a reserve for accidents. The moment someone cooks the seed stock, a beautiful field turns back into a foraging chore.

    Start with a small plot you can manage

    Once you have the Cultivator, break a small amount of ground somewhere you can defend, and leave the spacing each crop wants. Confirm one round grows to maturity before you expand. Different plants want different amounts of space and different biomes; see the Cultivator and crop table.

    Do not pave the whole yard the moment you unlock it. A bigger field costs more time in fencing, harvesting and replanting, and expanding is only worth it if your demand grew too. Solo saves in particular do well planning production around two or three dishes you actually eat.

    Keep seed stock and ingredients apart

    Some vegetables can be replanted to produce seeds; the turnip's seed mechanic is covered on the Turnip page. Put the crops you are keeping for seed in their own chest, with the purpose written in the chest name, so nobody cooks next round's capital.

    Hold back enough to replant what you already have, then cook the rest. When you have just unlocked a new crop, put more of the first harvest into propagation; once you have a buffer, raise the share you eat. That is a way to scale up, not a demand that you calculate yields precisely every time.

    Why barley will not grow back home

    Crops have biome requirements. Barley needs the Plains — see Barley. So a big Meadows base cannot be assumed to carry your farming through every stage. In the Mistlands stage, check the growth biome of whatever you are planting too, instead of judging by whether the ground is tilled.

    Near a biome border, check the biome name at your exact position before you plant. Making a dedicated field into its own outpost connected to the main base works well; you do not need to move an entire storeroom for one crop.

    Check unhealthy crops in order

    Read the in-game prompt first, then look at biome, spacing, structures overhead and nearby obstacles. Do not rip up and replant repeatedly the moment something does not grow. Noting where the healthy and unhealthy plants sit in the same plot usually makes it obvious whether the problem is space or environment.

    Fields need protection, but the protection must not break the crops' own conditions. Validate a small plot and then copy the layout; that wastes far less material than building one huge sealed greenhouse and reworking it.

    Make the farm serve your expeditions

    Every time you get home, run the same four actions: harvest, set seed aside, replant, cook. Keep finished meals near the route out the door, ingredients in the kitchen and seeds by the field. The point of farming is that you are never hunting for food at the moment you leave, not that the save turns into an endless harvesting job.
