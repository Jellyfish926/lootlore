    Shift At Midnight monsters

    Last verified 13 August 2026 · game version: 29 July 2026 patch · achievement rates re-read 13 August 2026

    Recently updated

    Every named threat in the game, and the thing that matters most about this list: the counterplay is different for every single one. There is no universal answer. The weapon that saves you from one of these is useless against another, and against one of them running is the only option that exists.

    The one rule that will save your life

    Identify before you commit. Two of the entries people search for as “monsters” are not monsters at all — one is an object, one is a customer — and two of the real threats cannot be killed with a weapon. Reaching for a gun as a reflex is how most first-run deaths happen.

    The bestiary

    Entity The baseline threat, and the only one you summon yourself: wave a doppelganger through the counter and it returns that same shift, in its real form, to hunt you. Blind, sound-tracking, killable — and guaranteed to come on blood-rain nights whatever you did at the till. Marionette The Shift 9 encounter, and the only one decided before the fight starts. A wind-up music box governs it: let the melody play three times without rewinding and you summon it. The 23 July patch cut its health and made the box louder, which changed this fight more than any other. The Dentist Shift 13, and the only threat with no counterplay at all. Immune to weapons, unbothered by traps. Every piece of correct advice about the Dentist is about running toward Sheriff Clyde. Demented It cannot move while you are looking at it — and it cannot be hurt while you are looking at it either. The confirmed solution is to break line of sight in a direction that walks it into a trap. 79.8% of players have managed it, so it is not the rare encounter it is often called. Shrieking Doll A low crawler that turns up during hunts. It dies to a few shots — the real cost is the noise you make killing it, which is why 89.8% of players have the achievement and far fewer come out of that hunt cleanly. Jack-in-the-Box Not a monster — an object. It is the music box that decides the Marionette encounter, and hold-E to rewind is the entire interaction. People search for it as an enemy, so it has its own page. Norbert Not a monster either — a customer. A gnome whose ID genuinely scans as fake, which makes him the single best test of whether you have understood the job. Shooting him is the mistake.

    Rakes — the forest threat

    Rakes are new. The 29 July 2026 patch added them to endless and post-story modes only, emerging from the forests around the station — so if you finished story mode before that date, you have not met one, and you will not meet one on a story run. Community reporting describes them as fast four-legged creatures that glow red and go for your customers rather than for you, with a customer’s scream as the tell that one has spawned. That behavioural description comes from a single source and is not independently confirmed; the patch note itself only names them and says where they appear. No reliable figures exist for their health, damage or spawn rate, so we are not publishing any.

    Doppelgangers are a category, not a creature

    The most common enemy in Shift At Midnight is the doppelganger, and it does not belong in the table above because it is not one monster. Doppelgangers pretend to be normal customers — appearance, voice, mannerisms, a plausible background — and your entire job at the counter is separating them from real people. They are handled through identification, not combat: the ID scanner, the N.E.T. database, the description box, and the gaps between what someone says and what the readings show.

    Getting it wrong is so routine that the game’s most-held achievement, at 96.9%, is for killing a customer. See the doppelganger identification guide for the seven categories of tell and the named examples worth memorising.

    Where the confusion comes from

    New players build a single rule — “fake ID means kill” — and it breaks in both directions. Norbert scans as fake and shooting him makes your shift worse, not better. The Dentist never comes through the door at all. A rule that treats the scanner as a threat detector will get you killed by the thing it was never pointed at.

    What actually changes as the shifts go on

    Story mode is 13 shifts, and the customers and events inside them are procedurally generated — which is why no credible night-by-night monster schedule exists, and why you should be suspicious of any site that publishes one. Three points in the run are fixed and documented:

    • Shift 9 — the Marionette mechanic comes online, announced in advance by a N.E.T. email.
    • After Shift 12 — the decision about whether to call Sheriff Clyde, which is the fork that decides your ending.
    • Shift 13 — the Dentist chase.

    Everything else escalates rather than switches: more doppelgangers, harder Entities, less slack. After the credits, Endless Mode unlocks and adds Rakes to the mix. See updates for what each patch has changed.
