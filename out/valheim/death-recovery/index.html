    Valheim Death Recovery: Get Your Gear

    Author Jellyfi · Published 2026-09-17 · Last checked 2026-09-17 · Checked on version 1.0.12

    Scope: Vanilla standard survival; world modifiers, mods and later patches can change the rules

    Language: English中文

    On this page

    1. Rebuild a minimum survival kit after respawning
    2. Scout the site before you close in
    3. Empty your pack to cut down interaction time
    4. Splitting roles for a group rescue
    5. Restock the rescue kit when you get back
    6. Sources
    7. Related guides

    Do not sprint back to your tombstone on an empty stomach. Stop and answer three questions first: where did the gear drop, is the original danger still there, and what resistance or transport do you need to reach it? A corpse run is a new operation, not a continuation of the fight you just lost. Under default rules your items stay in the tombstone waiting for you, as described in How to play. A world's death penalty settings can change the details.

    Rebuild a minimum survival kit after respawning

    Eat your spare food, get the Rested buff, and take the gear you actually need to reach the tombstone. Died somewhere cold? Sort out warmth first. Somewhere poisonous? Bring resistance. At sea? Arrange a boat before anything else. Do not skimp on one supply and create a second, harder-to-reach tombstone.

    Backup gear does not have to match your main set. It only has to support this one job: moving, and absorbing a small mistake. It is not there so you can clear the whole dungeon again in it.

    Scout the site before you close in

    Use the portal route you already have, and stop somewhere relatively safe. Look at enemies, weather and the ground; do not sprint in a straight line the moment the tombstone icon appears. If the enemies are still sitting there, pull them off, clear them, or wait for a teammate to open a window.

    If the tombstone landed on a slope, in deep water or next to something large, work out how you will move after you pick it up. Getting your things back and then being unable to leave still gets you killed. Above all, do not stop to reorganise a full inventory out in the open.

    Empty your pack to cut down interaction time

    Carry only what you need on the way out, leaving room for the gear you are recovering. When you arrive, grab everything first, then retreat somewhere safe to equip, sort and repair. A full tombstone recovery comes with a short buff, but do not treat that as immunity to everything.

    If you cannot carry it all in one go, secure the gear and key items the job needs and do the rest in batches. Do not stand there agonising over a stack of cheap material with an enemy already on top of you.

    Splitting roles for a group rescue

    One player pulls or handles the threats, one recovers, and anyone else holds the way back as needed. Agree on the tombstone location and the retreat direction before you start, rather than everyone chasing something different on arrival. Rescuers should bring their own supplies too, not rely on the potions in the dead player's pack.

    If two attempts fail in a row, change the plan: improve the approach route, add resistance, wait for teammates, or craft better backup gear first. Running the same naked dash again is not progress, just more chaos on site.

    Restock the rescue kit when you get back

    Repair your gear, replace food and spare materials, and note what actually killed you. Was it the route, the weather, stamina, or an attack you did not recognise? Fixing the losses without changing the cause tends to replay the same death on the next trip. The rescue corner at home gets refilled after every use, not the next time someone dies.
