3:T1b50,<p>The <strong>Wish Fountain</strong> is the Bunnyville device that lets you start a run with artifacts you already own — and the single decision that most separates a documented high-tier build from a lucky one. Every serious Sephiria build guide we have read opens with what it puts in the fountain, and the choices cluster into three kinds: the piece the build cannot start without, the legendary you refuse to wait for, and the survival item you would otherwise spend a floor hunting. This page explains the mechanic as officially published, then goes through what five builds actually carry in and the reasoning behind each.</p>
<p>Older community writing, including earlier versions of this site, calls it the "Wishing Fountain"; the official English name in patch notes is <strong>Wish Fountain</strong>.</p>

<h2>How capacity works — and where the numbers disagree</h2>
<p>The fountain's base capacity is <strong>4</strong> since the 0.10.0 update, which raised it from 3. Extra capacity comes from the <strong>Willpower</strong> talent: +1 at level 5 and a larger bonus at level 20. How large is where the official record contradicts itself — the same 0.10.0 announcement says the talent's maximum was "reduced from 6 to 5" and, two lines later, lists the level-20 effect as "increase reduced from 6 to 4", which would make the total 4 + 5 rather than a maximum of 5. TEAM HORAY has not reconfirmed either figure in the 1.0 patches. The <a href="/artifacts/artifacts-and-tablets/">artifact system</a> page quotes both lines verbatim, and the <a href="/guides/talents/">talents page</a> prints the per-level values with the same flag; we do not pick a winner.</p>
<p>What is not in dispute: <strong>Willpower 20 is the talent that turns the fountain into a build tool.</strong> Three of the five guides below put 20 points there for that reason alone, and the Korean Blizzard Hammer guide says it in one sentence — Willpower 20 "so the two artifacts can be carried to the next adventure."</p>
<p>Capacity is measured in artifacts, not rarity, so a legendary costs the same slot as a common. That is why the carry-in lists below are short and almost all legendary or build-critical.</p>
<h2>What five builds carry in</h2>

<p>Three patterns fall out of that table.</p>
<p><strong>Carry the enabler, not the damage.</strong> Furnace, Frostium Ring and Simple Contract do no damage themselves; each one unlocks a family of artifacts that would otherwise be dead weight. A carried enabler also biases the drop pool — artifacts in the bag raise the chance of their own tag appearing, which the fire Blade guide puts at roughly +100% for the combo bonus and up to +75% more with a Carrot-modified Fruit Skewer.</p>
<p><strong>Carry the legendary you cannot afford to find late.</strong> Crown of Humility is the model: a build that keys off it is a different build for every floor it is missing.</p>
<p><strong>Survival items are a judgement call, not a default.</strong> Two guides warn that lifesteal taken before defence loses runs; the spellbook author turns the same warning into a costume decision instead.</p>
<h2>Carried items and the Side Bag</h2>
<p>Since 1.0.19 you also have a <strong>Side Bag</strong> for spare items, and its rule matters for fountain planning: <strong>items in the Side Bag are inactive</strong>. The fire Blade guide uses this deliberately — the Red-Clothed Cat's starting fire bolt goes into the Side Bag so it does not pollute the artifact pool until the mandatory pieces are in. A carried artifact you want to <em>hold</em> but not <em>activate</em> yet can be parked the same way. 1.0.23 fixed starting items from a costume or talent being kept after you parked them in the Side Bag and switched loadout, so the bag behaves consistently now.</p>
<h2>What the fountain is not</h2>
<p>It is not a rarity filter, and it does not replace the <a href="/guides/destiny-inscription/">Destiny Inscription</a> economy — Sapphires, dice, slots and the Willpower points that raise capacity all still come from the tree. The <strong>Chest of Possibilities</strong> (2 Sapphires for three merchant items, reroll cost doubling) and the <strong>Mystic Pot</strong>, re-worked in 1.0.19 into a converter, are the other two tools for shaping a bag, and they are documented on the artifact page rather than here.</p>
<p>If you carry only one thing, the guides' answer is consistent: whatever your weapon line <em>cannot start without</em>.
