3:T2943,<p><em>Last reviewed: 1 September 2026 · Newest patch at review time: 1.0.30 (20 August 2026).</em> TEAM HORAY's own 1.0 figures: <strong>about 300 Artifacts, about 70 Tablets, over 30 Potions.</strong> The 1.0.19 patch alone added 19 artifacts and 7 tablets. This page is about where those come from and how they relate — the mechanical detail of <em>placement</em> lives on the <a href="/guides/inventory-grid/">inventory grid</a> page.</p>
<p>The relationship in one line: <strong>artifacts do the work, tablets decide how much of it they do.</strong> An artifact sitting on a cell with no tablet coverage is at level 0.</p>

<h2>Where artifacts come from</h2>
<p>There are more sources than most players realise, and several are one-time-only pools worth deliberately farming:</p>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Source</th><th>Notes</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Artifact node</strong></td><td>The obvious one — a room with a choice</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Level Up node</strong></td><td>Rewards include artifacts</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Mini-boss kill</strong></td><td>The grey-skull node modifier; always drops an artifact</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Fixed shop</strong></td><td>Every floor's starting room except floor 1</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Shop node / Wandering Merchant</strong></td><td>The rat merchant bargains ~10% better than fixed shops</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Secret rooms</strong></td><td>Cracked walls; artifacts are the rarest of the possible rewards</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Blood Donation</strong></td><td>33% of current HP to the Iron Maiden</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Collin</strong></td><td>500 Leaf for <em>Hiring Crest "Collin"</em>, a Companion-combo artifact</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Dimensional Rift</strong></td><td>Sapphires; <strong>8 artifacts + 1 tablet</strong> unlock here permanently and nowhere else</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Wandering Witch</strong></td><td>Very rare; <strong>11 artifacts + 2 tablets exclusive to her</strong>, sold cheap</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Chest of Possibilities</strong></td><td>2 Sapphires, reroll cost doubles 4 → 8 → 16</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Wish Fountain</strong></td><td>Carry chosen items into a run; base capacity 4 — see the capacity note below</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Tree Roots</strong></td><td>One Miracle per run, several of which grant a starting item</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>Note that spelling: patch notes and item text use <strong>"Collin"</strong>, not "Colin". The fountain's official English name is <strong>"Wish Fountain"</strong> — older community writing (including an earlier version of this page) calls it the "Wishing Fountain".</p>
<p><strong>The capacity numbers do not reconcile, so here are all of them.</strong> The <a href="https://steamcommunity.com/games/2436940/announcements/detail/1819386365106847" rel="nofollow noopener" target="_blank">0.10.0 update</a> says, verbatim: <em>"Wish Fountain: Base capacity increased from 3 to 4, and the maximum capacity obtainable through the talent 'Willpower' has been reduced from 6 to 5."</em> The same announcement then lists Willpower's level-5 effect as <em>"Wish Fountain capacity increased by 1"</em> and its level-20 effect as <em>"Wish Fountain capacity increase reduced from 6 to 4"</em> — which sums to a base 4 plus 5 from the talent, not a maximum of 5. Our <a href="/guides/talents/">talents page</a> prints the per-level lines; this page previously printed "up to +4 more". TEAM HORAY has not re-confirmed any of these numbers in the 1.0 patches, so we print the official text as published and flag that it disagrees with itself rather than pick a winner.</p>
<h2>Rarity and levels</h2>
<p>Artifacts have rarity tiers, and patch notes move items between them regularly — <em>Golden Maple Leaf</em> went Common → Rare, <em>Water Elemental's Paw Wax</em> went Legendary → Rare, <em>Solis Dekri</em> went Rare → Legendary. The tier names that appear in official text are <strong>Common, Uncommon, Rare, Advanced, Legendary</strong>, with a rarity indicator in the top-right of each item.</p>
<p>We will flag one caveat rather than paper over it: "Advanced" appears in patch notes both as an <em>upgrade</em> from Rare and as a <em>downgrade</em> to Rare. That is almost certainly one Korean grade translated inconsistently across builds, and it means the published ladder cannot be stated with confidence.</p>
<p>Levels are separate from rarity. Most artifacts scale through discrete levels with per-level stat arrays — <em>Charm of Intelligence</em> runs 3/4/6/8 Ice Damage, <em>Shield Earrings</em> runs 6/12/18/24 HP. Max level varies per artifact and is shown as empty stars above the item. <strong>Some artifacts have no levels at all.</strong></p>
<p>This is why a bag full of level-0 artifacts underperforms a bag with four maxed ones. A player build guide puts it bluntly about its own core items: not maxing them <em>"hurts the build drastically."</em></p>
<h2>The conversion devices</h2>
<p>Two objects let you launder items you do not want.</p>
<p><strong>Mystic Pot.</strong> Reworked in 1.0: it now converts <strong>1 artifact into a different artifact of the same rarity, or 2 artifacts into one of higher rarity.</strong> It is reusable, and turns up as a stage reward, in hidden rooms and occasionally in merchant rooms. (It originally did something else entirely — in its 0.10.9 form it turned artifacts <em>into tablets</em>. Guides written before 1.0 describe that older behaviour.)</p>
<p><strong>Tablet Combiner.</strong> A random object that synthesises two tablets into one carrying both effects. It appears in every Safe Zone after Wasteland, next to the merchant, costs <strong>200 Leaf</strong>, and works once per combiner.</p>
<h2>Special artifact categories</h2>
<p><strong>Bond Artifacts</strong> are unique artifacts that <strong>connect two combos</strong> — roughly a dozen at introduction, five more added later. Their drop rule was loosened: they enter the drop table when <strong>one</strong> of the related combos is active, where originally both were required.</p>
<p><strong>Activated Artifacts</strong> are ones you bind to a key in the skill window and use manually, rather than passives.</p>
<p><strong>Heavy Heart</strong> is the odd one out: a Hard Mode modifier that starts you with an artifact that has no effect and cannot be discarded. It exists purely to cost you a slot.</p>
<h2>Tablets, briefly</h2>
<p>Tablets are the emitters. Each occupies one cell and projects buff values onto specific other cells — discrete offsets, a whole row/column/diagonal, or in one case a board-wide chessboard pattern. The community's open dataset catalogues 62 of them with full mechanics, including <em>Requital</em> (+3 to its entire row, the strongest line effect), <em>Conviction</em> (+5 to a single cell, the strongest point effect), and the four edge-anchored ones that do nothing unless placed on the correct grid edge.</p>
<p>They come from Tablet nodes, shops, and the Defense talent at level 5, which hands you a <strong>Foundation</strong> tablet at the start of every run. They are gated content — tablets must be unlocked in Destiny Inscription before tablet nodes appear at all, which is why that node is the first thing to buy.</p>
<p>Balance moves on tablets in 1.0.19: Nurture and Trick both dropped from Advanced to Common rarity, Competition's downward bonus went +2 → +3, Wave's diagonal went +2 → +3, and Daydream was reworked from eight weak cells to <strong>+2 on each of the four diagonals</strong>.</p>
<h2>What "empower them with Tablets" means concretely</h2>
<p>The store's phrasing is <em>"Collect and arrange Artifacts and empower them with the different Tablets."</em> Concretely:</p>
<ol><li>You place an artifact on a cell.</li><li>Every tablet whose pattern reaches that cell contributes its value, positive or negative.</li><li>The sum is that artifact's level for as long as it sits there.</li></ol>
<p>Move the artifact one cell and its level changes. That is the entire loop, and it is why the inventory screen is where Sephiria runs are actually won or lost.</p>
<h2>Two corrections to circulating claims</h2>
<p><strong>"Wisdom level 10 gives +2 combo" is not right for 1.0.</strong> What Wisdom 10 does is unlock <em>[Unique] Stacking</em>: duplicates get offered, and taking one adds a star to the copy you already hold. There is a combo interaction, but TEAM HORAY explicitly stated it was <strong>not</strong> extended to combo-changing artifacts (Chalcedony Key, White Paper) for technical reasons — and separately fixed a bug where the UI <em>displayed</em> a combo increase that was never applied.</p>
<p><strong>"Völuspá" is not a confirmed artifact name.</strong> It does not appear in any Steam announcement, on the store page, or in the community dataset. It circulates widely on auto-generated wikis. Until it turns up in a primary source, we are not listing it.</p>
<h2>Two artifacts changed in the August patches</h2>
<p>The launch-era rule that no artifact balance had moved since 1.0.19 expired in August: <strong>1.0.26 fixed the Green Sawblade</strong> so its buff stacks correctly when 30 or more MP is spent at once, and <strong>1.0.30 gave the Frozen Bow arrows that pass through walls</strong> — a real capability buff, marked as community-requested in TEAM HORAY's notes. The same patches fixed Tablet Imprinting eating Tablets whose activation conditions were unmet, and barred undiscardable Tablets from the Tablet Combiner. Details on the <a href="/patch-notes/1-0-25-to-1-0-30/">1.0.25–1.0.30 patch page</a>.</p>
<h2>The counting problem</h2>
<p>The developer says "about 300 artifacts" and "about 70 tablets." A community data audit at 1.0.24/1.0.25 counts <strong>236 artifacts and 92 tablets</strong>. Those do not reconcile, and we are not going to invent a methodology that makes them.
