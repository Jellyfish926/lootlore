DragonSword Cooking: Boil, Grill, Slice, 73 Recipes

Last verified 2026-08-10

There is no natural HP regeneration in DragonSword: Awakening. Out of combat you heal at a Goddess Statue of Organa. In combat, only Lute has an on-demand heal — and you can carry just two equipped consumables at a time.

That is the context that makes cooking a core system rather than a pleasant diversion. Food is how you heal during a fight, and there is no substitute.

The three methods

MethodWhat it produces
Grill / RoastHP or Stamina restoration
BoilBuffs — attack up, defense up. Some boil recipes yield teas that act as revival items
SliceReduces stamina consumption while flying, swimming, dashing, climbing and clambering

The Slice category is easy to dismiss and should not be. Traversal in Orbis is stamina-gated across five separate movement types, and the jump-puzzle content (Trial of Mist) is where players report the most friction. A stamina dish is the difference between clearing a climb and falling off it.

Boil's revival teas are the more important discovery for combat. With one healer in the roster and two consumable slots, a revival item in one of those slots changes what a wipe costs.

How recipes work

You do not buy or find recipes. You discover them by experimenting. Combine ingredients, and if the combination is valid, the dish is added to your cookbook permanently.

There are 73 recipes, or 74 if you count the "Mysterious Dish." A single dish usually has several valid ingredient combinations, which is what makes experimentation viable rather than a lottery — you are not hunting one exact triple.

A Cookbook filter was added for the full release specifically in response to demo feedback, so if you played the June demo and found the list unmanageable, that has been addressed.

Ingredient quality maps directly to dish quality

The star rating of your ingredients drives the star rating of the dish. The rule players have worked out: three 4-star ingredients yield a 5-star dish.

That is a straightforward optimisation once you know it. Do not cook with whatever is at the top of your bag — sort by star rating first, and save your high-star ingredients for the buff dishes you actually want at maximum strength.

Ingredients that are not used in anything

Worth knowing so you stop hoarding them: pistachios, cheese, sugar, carrot with white flowers, pepper, tofu, polished rice, almonds, butter and salt are not required by any recipe.

They still occupy inventory. If you are short on space before a long expedition, those are the safe things to leave behind.

Where cooking happens

You cook from the field and from the menu, and every hero has their own cooking animation and cinematic — Hound13 has patched them individually, which tells you how much bespoke work went in. Patch 1.0.7 fixed Reina's weapon clipping through her arm during the [Boil] and [Slice] actions, and a separate bug where characters showed a default pose during cooking cinematics. Adventure Quest 59 had a bug where Johnny's cooking icon displayed as Lute's.

Bugs fixed, and one thing to check

Radish Soup was always crafted at 1-star quality regardless of ingredients, until patch 1.0.7. If you tried it before that and wrote it off, try again.

More importantly: a launch-day patch reset some players' discovered recipes — specifically players who had been on a pre-launch build. Cooking recipes, mini-game records, titles, Karma Codex entries and Sudden Mission discovery were all affected. If your cookbook looks emptier than you remember, that is the likely cause and the recipes are re-discoverable.

How cooking fits the healing economy after 1.0.7

Patch 1.0.7 materially changed the surrounding systems, and it is worth understanding what it did and did not fix:

Fixed: the Goddess Statue of Organa usage limit was removed entirely — statues are now unlimited. Dungeon entry and wipe recovery now start you at 100% HP with all fallen characters revived, replacing the previous respawn at low health.

Not fixed: there is still no [Retry]. Hound13 added [Quick Entry] for multiplayer content and explicitly stated that [Retry] was delayed to the following week. The complaint that generated this — a 59-hour review describing four consecutive loading screens after a failure — was about the retry loop specifically.

So cooking's role has shifted slightly. Before 1.0.7, food was compensating for a hostile recovery system. Now it is doing the job it was designed for: in-fight healing and pre-fight buffs.

A practical routine

  1. Gather while traversing. Ingredients are scattered across the continent and you will walk past most of them. There is no separate farming loop.
  2. Sort by star rating before cooking. Three 4-star ingredients is the target.
  3. Fill one consumable slot with a heal, one with a buff or revival tea. Two slots is the hard limit; decide deliberately rather than by whatever was cooked last.
  4. Keep a Slice dish for traversal content. Trial of Mist and the sliding-pillar puzzles are where stamina actually runs out.
  5. Experiment with leftovers. Discovery is permanent and there is no penalty for a failed combination beyond the ingredients.

What is not published

The full recipe table — which ingredients make which dish — does not exist in any source we consider reliable. It circulates on auto-generated wikis and in one YouTube video; we have not transcribed it because we cannot verify it, and a wrong ingredient list wastes materials that take real time to gather.

What is sourced here is the method breakdown, the 73/74 recipe count, the quality rule, the unused-ingredient list, and the patch history. That is the part you can act on without risk.
