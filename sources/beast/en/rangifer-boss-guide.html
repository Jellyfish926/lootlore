Rangifer: Chapter 1's First Real Wall

Rangifer is the deer-shaped apex Malefact that opens Beast of Reincarnation's boss roster, and it is the single biggest filter in the whole campaign — a little over half of everyone who starts the game ever seals it. This page pulls together everything our launch coverage has established about the fight: when you meet it, the Flame weakness the weapon system is built to exploit, the one attack that pays your bills, the arts you walk away with, and where the numbers on this site disagree with each other.

Where you meet it

Rangifer belongs to Chapter 1, the chapter titled Seedling, and you do not fight it once. Our boss list records three separate encounters: a first meeting with the boss listed at Level 9, a second at Level 12 still inside Chapter 1, and a rematch far later in Chapter 5 (Branching Paths) with the boss scaled up to Level 32. That structure matters more than it looks. The game is not throwing a one-off gate at you; it is introducing a pattern it intends to test you on three times across roughly a third of the campaign, which is why time spent learning the animation set here is time you get back twice over.

Chapter 1 sits in the Kanto stretch of the journey, the opening pair of chapters our walkthrough groups together as the steepest part of the player funnel. The beginner guide calls Rangifer a giant deer-like Malefact nicknamed “The Prowling Nushi” and places the encounter at around player level 6–7. Read those two numbers side by side and you get the shape of the fight rather than a contradiction: you arrive a couple of levels under the thing you are hitting, and the deficit is meant to be closed with technique rather than XP.

Weakness — and what you can actually equip

Rangifer is the one boss on this site where the weakness is corroborated three separate times. The boss list records it as weak to Flame; our review lists the same in its roster summary; and the weapons page uses the fight as its worked example of the elemental system, stating that a Fire-enchanted setup paired with one of Koo's fire-based Bloom Arts breaks the boss's poise considerably faster than unaugmented melee. If you only ever element-match one fight in the entire game, this is the one the game itself is nudging you toward.

That is also why Rangifer is the cleanest place to practise: Fire is one of only three enchantable elements the weapons page confirms in the shipped build (Fire, Lightning, Wet), so the counter is something you can actually slot rather than something you have to hope drops. Arrows and bolts take up to three elemental slots and a katana can carry an element of its own, which means a bow build can arrive at the arena pre-configured. The beginner guide makes the same point in blunter terms: Rangifer alone will teach you why Fire matters.

One honest caveat on the Bloom Art. The beginner guide notes that a fire-based art exists and is worth using here, but also that the exact art's name varies between accounts, so it tells you to confirm the name in your own menu rather than trust a printed label. We have not resolved that name anywhere on this site, so treat it as not confirmed and read it off your own command list.

The antler sweep is the fight

Two of our pages independently single out the same move. The beginner guide describes Rangifer's telegraphed horizontal antler sweep as your FP piggy bank, and the walkthrough's boss line says to deflect the antler sweeps to bank Florescence Points and then spend them on Koo during the boss's recovery. Put together, the fight has an unusually explicit intended solution: the boss hands you a slow, readable, repeatable attack, and the entire economy of the game is waiting for you to block it correctly.

The economy itself is documented on the Koo guide: clean parries are the primary source of Florescence Points, FP banks into a shared pool, and the command menu you spend it from slows time without pausing the fight, so Emma still has to hold her spacing while you choose. The same page's advice about when to spend applies here more than almost anywhere — Koo is the stagger engine, and the highest-value moment to fire an art is when the enemy's Down Gauge is close to breaking, converting it into a stun rather than a few ordinary hits. Against a boss you are meant to parry dozens of times, that gauge fills often.

The corollary, and the reason so many players bounce: a dodge-only style starves the entire second half of your kit. If Koo feels useless in this fight, the Koo guide's diagnosis is that you are dodging when you should be deflecting, not that your build is wrong.

What sealing it gives you

Rangifer is the most generous boss in the first act. The boss list records its full reward set as the Nushi Skill Sakura Storm, the art Sakura: Javelin (which upgrades to a second rank on a later encounter), and both a Blade Art and a Finisher in the Sakura line. The Koo guide confirms Sakura Storm as the first Nushi Skill in the documented unlock order, and the site's home page uses it as the example of how boss-by-boss absorption expands the pair's shared kit.

A small curiosity for anyone who reaches Chapter 9 later: the moveset table on our Crimson Malefact guide lists an attack also called Sakura: Javelin — there, a fast spear throw that is easier to parry than to dodge on reaction. Our pages do not establish any mechanical relationship between the two, so read the shared name as a naming convention rather than a link between the fights.

Loadout notes

You reach Rangifer before most of the systems on this site are open to you, which shapes what is worth preparing. The Cleanse Walker hub — the Workbench, the merchant Brad, and the Communicator that lets you redistribute points — only unlocks near the end of Chapter 2 per our glossary, so a bad stat spread here is something you live with for a while rather than something you undo the same evening.

  • Fund the first upgrade before the fight, not after. Our Amber farming guide names Cliff Facing Tower in the Ogouchi Buffer Zone as a Chapter 1 sweep worth roughly 150 XP and 1,500 Amber, and its advice for that spot is specific: kill the Golems quickly, because they can finish off the Deer before you reach it and cost you part of the payout.
  • Spend Amber on weapon enhancement first. The same guide's priority order puts Workbench upgrades ahead of anything else early, because a stronger weapon compounds into every later fight.
  • Spirit Stones that pay you for parrying. The Spirit Stones guide lists Entangled Parry, which has a chance to restore 0.2 of the Entanglement Gauge and 1 FP on a parry (scaling from 10% to 50% across five levels). On a boss whose designed solution is repeated deflection, that trigger fires more often here than in ordinary play.

How many players get past it

Rangifer has its own achievement, Rangifer's End, and it is the clearest difficulty measurement on the site. Our trophy page records it at 53.4% of Steam owners on its August 31 read; the achievement tracker, which re-reads the same statistics weekly, shows 53.7% on September 7. Both are honest snapshots of a figure that drifts, not rival claims — but it does mean you should quote the date along with the number.

Either way the conclusion is the same and it is worth internalising before your tenth attempt: close to half of everyone who owns the game never seals this boss. The trophy page's read of the whole ladder is that the early chapters filter out almost half the audience while nearly everyone who clears Chapter 7 goes on to finish. Being stuck here is the statistically normal experience, and the difficulty guide documents that selectable settings exist precisely so a wall like this does not end a run.

What this page does not claim

We have no tested attack-by-attack table for Rangifer on this site. Beyond the antler sweep, no individual move of its moveset is documented in our coverage, and we are not going to invent one — the Crimson Malefact page carries a full moveset table only because a tested breakdown existed for it. Likewise, the Chapter 5 rematch at Level 32 is recorded as an appearance but its differences from the Chapter 1 version are not established anywhere here. Assume the pattern carries over; do not assume nothing has been added.

Rangifer FAQ

What level should I be for Rangifer?

The boss is listed at Level 9 on the first encounter and Level 12 on the second. The beginner guide puts players at roughly level 6–7 when they arrive, so expect to be slightly under — the fight is tuned around parrying, not out-levelling.

Is Rangifer weak to Fire or to Flame?

Those are the same weakness under two labels. The boss list writes it as Flame; the weapons page writes the equipment side as Fire, which is one of the three confirmed enchantable elements.

Do I have to fight Rangifer more than once?

Yes. Two encounters in Chapter 1 and a Chapter 5 rematch at Level 32 are recorded on the boss list, and Rangifer is one of four bosses our walkthrough flags as returning at higher levels.

What do I get for beating it?

The Nushi Skill Sakura Storm, the Sakura: Javelin art and its later upgrade, and a Blade Art and Finisher in the Sakura line — the largest reward bundle of any early fight.
