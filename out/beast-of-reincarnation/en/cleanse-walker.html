    The Cleanse Walker: Workbench, Brad and the Respec Terminal

    Compiled from this site's own launch guides · Last reviewed September 11, 2026

    Language: English日本語FrançaisEspañolDeutschItaliano

    Systems · Hub

    Beast of Reincarnation has no town. It has the Cleanse Walker — one mobile hub carrying the Workbench, the merchant Brad and the Communicator that refunds your points — and it does not open until near the end of Chapter 2. This page collects what each service does, the order worth spending Amber in, the duplication trap to avoid, and how much of the early game you play without any of it.

    What the Cleanse Walker is

    Beast of Reincarnation has no town. What it has instead is the Cleanse Walker — a hub our glossary defines as home to the Workbench for weapon enhancement, Spirit Stone duplication, and the merchant Brad. Our builds guide describes the same place as your mobile base, where you bank progress, save and upgrade, and our walkthrough files it under progression as where you save, level up and manage gear between chapters.

    Everything you can buy, upgrade, duplicate or undo in this game runs through one location, and it is not open from the start. That combination is the reason this page exists: almost every piece of build advice on this site quietly assumes the Cleanse Walker, and the first stretch of the campaign is played without it.

    When it opens — and what that costs you

    Two of our pages pin the timing. Our glossary says the hub unlocks near the end of Chapter 2, and our Amber farming guide gives the same chapter for the Cleanse Walker Communicator specifically — the terminal that resets Emma's or Koo's attribute and skill points.

    Line that up against the boss roster on our boss list and the shape of the early game changes. Before the hub opens you have already fought, at minimum, Rangifer twice (Levels 9 and 12), the Nue Malefact (Level 16) and Lacerta twice (Levels 13 and 16) — five boss encounters and a chapter and a half of levelling, with no way to respec any of it. Our Rangifer guide states the consequence plainly: a bad stat spread at that point is something you live with for a while rather than something you undo the same evening.

    This is also why our beginner guide and the early-game funnel data belong in the same conversation. Our trophy page reads the achievement ladder as the early chapters filtering out almost half the audience — and those are precisely the chapters played without a respec option, a merchant or a Workbench. The game gets considerably more forgiving the moment the Cleanse Walker opens.

    The three services

    ServiceWhat it doesWhere our coverage discusses it
    WorkbenchWeapon enhancement, and Spirit Stone duplicationAmber farming guide, Spirit Stones guide
    BradMerchant: buys and sells Spirit Stones, recipes and manuals for AmberGlossary, Spirit Stones guide, Amber farming guide
    CommunicatorResets Emma's or Koo's attribute and skill pointsAmber farming guide, builds guide

    The Communicator is the one that changes how you play rather than what you carry. Our builds guide records that points can be redistributed and that early picks are not permanent, and our Koo guide extends the same freedom to Koo's separate skill tree — SP can be refunded, so you are free to respec and experiment as new enemies force new answers. The Amber farming guide's read is that attribute resets are cheap enough to use experimentally once unlocked, so a build direction that is not clicking is a reversible mistake rather than a run-ending one.

    That freedom is what makes the elemental routing on our weakness guide practical. If you can retune between chapters, arriving at a boss with the wrong build is a detour, not a wall.

    Brad, and what he is worth

    Our glossary files Brad as the merchant stationed at the Cleanse Walker who buys and sells Spirit Stones, recipes and manuals for Amber. Our Amber farming guide adds Skill Ambers to what he stocks, alongside wandering merchants elsewhere in the world.

    Two of those stock categories matter more than they look. Manuals are the gate on arrow and bolt types — our achievement tracker records the achievement Manual Maniac as “Obtain the manuals for every arrow and bolt type,” sitting at 12.9% on its 7 September read, and our trophy page flags it as an optional unlock that is easy to walk past. If you intend to answer bosses with ammunition rather than melee, Brad's inventory is part of that plan.

    Selling is the other direction, and our Spirit Stones guide has one firm rule for it: keep higher-rarity stones, sell the common ones. Rare stones are genuinely harder to find in the overworld, while common ones can simply be rebought from a merchant if you change your mind. Sell when you need Amber urgently, not as a habit — a stone you offload today may be the one a build you have not tried yet wants.

    The duplication trap

    The Workbench's duplication function looks like a way to stock up and is not. Our Spirit Stones guide is unambiguous about the two limits: duplication costs noticeably more Amber than the stone would cost to find or buy again, and you can only duplicate stones already in your inventory, so it never unlocks anything new. The guide's own framing is that it is a late-game convenience for a stone you have settled on permanently.

    Our Amber farming guide lists duplicating Spirit Stones early among its four common mistakes, for a second reason worth its own sentence: it locks you into a build choice at the exact point in the game when you have the least information about which build you will end up running. Wait until after you have used the Communicator at least once.

    What to spend on, in order

    Our Amber farming guide gives a priority order, and it resolves cleanly into a sequence for the hub:

    1. Weapon enhancement at the Workbench, first. The reasoning is compounding: upgraded gear makes every fight afterward faster and safer, which speeds up the exploration loop that earns you more Amber. Our Rangifer guide applies this to the earliest possible case — fund the first upgrade before the boss, not after.
    2. Manuals and recipes from Brad when you see them, because they gate systems rather than boosting numbers, and our trophy page's evidence is that they are easy to miss entirely.
    3. Attribute resets via the Communicator freely, once unlocked — cheap enough to treat as experimentation rather than a commitment.
    4. Spirit Stone duplication last, and only for a stone you are certain about.

    Funding all of that is an exploration problem rather than a grinding one. Our Amber farming guide's central finding is that sweeping each new area with Koo active — following his cues to hidden strongboxes, breaking Amber Crystals on your route, collecting Amber Butterflies — outpaces parking at one enemy spawn. The two named early spots it records are Cliff Facing Tower in the Ogouchi Buffer Zone (Chapter 1, roughly 150 XP and 1,500 Amber per sweep) and Flower Field Tower in the Great Wall Terraces (Chapter 3, roughly 180 XP and 1,700 Amber).

    A naming note

    This site writes the hub's name two ways. Our glossary, Spirit Stones guide and Amber farming guide all use Cleanse Walker; our builds guide writes Cleansing Walker. Nothing in our coverage suggests these are two places — the described function is identical in both — so read them as one location under two spellings. We use Cleanse Walker here because it is the majority spelling across the site and the one the glossary standardised on, but we have not established which form the in-game text uses, so treat the canonical spelling as not confirmed.

    What this page does not claim

    We have no prices. No page on this site carries an Amber cost for a weapon enhancement, a respec, a duplication or any item in Brad's stock — “duplication costs more than rebuying” and “resets are cheap” are the only two relative statements our sources support, and neither is a number. We also do not know how the hub moves or whether its services expand as the campaign goes on, what the Workbench's weapon enhancement actually raises, or whether Brad's inventory changes by chapter. Our Amber farming guide additionally flags that the acquisition method for Skill Ambers specifically was not established in its sources, so treat that item as not confirmed too.

    Cleanse Walker FAQ

    When do I unlock the Cleanse Walker?

    Near the end of Chapter 2, per our glossary; our Amber farming guide dates the Communicator terminal to the same point. You fight roughly five boss encounters before it opens.

    Can I respec before then?

    Nothing in our coverage says so. The Communicator is the only reset mechanism this site documents, and it lives at the hub — so treat Chapter 1 and most of Chapter 2 as committed.

    Who is Brad?

    The merchant at the Cleanse Walker, who buys and sells Spirit Stones, recipes, manuals and Skill Ambers for Amber. He also appears in the story: our walkthrough lists Brad and Kagura as companions who join along the way.

    Should I duplicate Spirit Stones?

    Rarely, and late. Duplication costs more than finding or rebuying the stone, only works on stones you already own, and commits you to a build. Our Spirit Stones guide treats it as a convenience, not a strategy.

    Is the Cleanse Walker the same as the Cleansing Walker?

    They describe the same hub, with the same Workbench, merchant and reset terminal. The spelling difference is ours, across two pages, rather than a distinction in the game.

    What should I buy first?

    A weapon enhancement. Our Amber farming guide puts Workbench upgrades ahead of everything else because they compound into every later fight — and the earlier you fund one, the longer it compounds.
