3:T175e,<p>Only <strong>16.3%</strong> of Sephiria players have seen the ending. This page is about being in that sixth: how to build a run that survives the chapter 6 finale, and what the game does and does not tell you about the endings on the other side of it.</p>
<p>The boss himself — <strong>Qliphoth</strong>, fought on floor 7 through Revival, True Form and Final Form — is mapped with the rest of the roster on the <a href="/bosses/chapters-and-bosses/">boss list</a>. This page is the preparation and aftermath; that page is the who-and-where.</p>

<h2>What makes the finale different from every other floor</h2>
<p>Three structural facts, from the one dedicated fight guide in circulation plus TEAM HORAY's own patch notes:</p>
<p><strong>The phases chain without a pause.</strong> Form transitions are not checkpoints and there is no mid-fight breather to drink through. Whatever HP and MP you carry across a transition is your starting position for the next form — budget resources for a marathon, not three sprints.</p>
<p><strong>The arena tightens late.</strong> The closing stretch shrinks your usable space while persistent hazard zones stay live, so dodge patterns that worked all run — long dashes through open floor — stop fitting. Builds that rely on distance decay here; builds that can fight inside a phone booth do not.</p>
<p><strong>His combos chain off a single hit.</strong> One clipped attack can cascade. This is why the guide's preparation advice is phrased defensively even for damage builds.</p>
<h2>The preparation rule of thumb</h2>
<p>The Medium guide's formula is worth quoting because it prices each stat honestly: enough <strong>defense to survive two hits</strong>, enough <strong>recovery that one mistake is repairable</strong>, and <em>everything else</em> into damage. Not a tank build — a build where an error costs a correction instead of the run.</p>
<p>The same logic favours <strong>sustained damage over burst</strong>. Long phases with no pauses reward the build that never stops hitting, not the one that empties everything into ten seconds. If your damage graph is a spike followed by cooldowns, the second half of each form is where the fight will beat you.</p>
<p>Worth knowing before you judge your own attempt: the fight was <strong>harder before 1.0.21</strong>. That hotfix cut the phase 1 radial bullet spread and took a third of the HP off phase 2's Pillar Summoner minions. Advice written in the launch week describes a slightly nastier fight than the current one.</p>
<h2>The endings</h2>
<p>What is verifiable: chapter 6 and "a new ending" arrived together in update 1.0.19 — the phrasing itself confirming the game has <strong>more than one</strong>. Reaching an ending at all is the <em>Journey's End</em> achievement (16.3%), and a separate chain of hidden achievements — <em>Soul Plan</em> (30.8%), <em>Hero's Guise</em> (27.6%), <em>Temple of Evil</em> (17.1%), <em>Seal</em> (16.4%) — tracks what the community treats as the <strong>true ending route</strong>.</p>
<p>What the chain's numbers reveal: nearly a third of players start it, and the big filter is between Hero's Guise and Temple of Evil — after that, everyone finishes. So the route's early steps are accessible; the commitment is in its back half.</p>
<p>What we will not print: step-by-step conditions. Steam hides these achievements' descriptions, and no source we checked documents the exact triggers in a way that can be verified — most pages that claim to are reworded guesses. Between the <a href="/guides/achievements/">achievements list</a> and this page you have every number that is actually public; the specific triggers you will have to discover, or bring us evidence for via the <a href="/contact/">contact page</a>.</p>
<h2>After the credits</h2>
<p><strong>Hard Mode.</strong> Finishing the game opens Root's Retreat — thirty escalating stages with their own achievements at stages 1, 15 and 30 (45.2%, 30.8% and 19.6% of players respectively). The <a href="/guides/hard-mode/">Hard Mode page</a> covers the modifier stack; note that its stage rewards include talent points, so the mode funds its own climb.</p>
<p><strong>The Pocket Dimension changes jobs.</strong> During the campaign it is a curiosity (and the 77.2% <em>Where the Blue Being Lives</em> achievement); an early-access-era developer-community exchange described it as becoming an <strong>artifact-unlock system after completion</strong>. That description predates 1.0, so treat the details as historical — but if you cleared the game and are wondering where new artifacts come from, that is the room to check.</p>
<p><strong>No New Game Plus exists</strong> as far as any source documents — the post-game loop is Hard Mode depth, the true-ending route if you skipped it, and the <a href="/guides/costumes/">costume</a> and achievement chases. If TEAM HORAY's 3-4 day patch cadence eventually adds more, the patch-notes section will have it before this page does.</p>
<h2>Checked against 1.0.30</h2>
<p>Three fixes to the Chapter 6 finale landed on 13 August (<strong>1.0.26</strong>): the boss no longer remains invincible when stunned mid-teleport, no longer stands still when stunned at the start of certain patterns, and the Give Up button is usable again after the battle. Rejoining a multiplayer game after the boss was already defeated no longer blocks progression, and <strong>1.0.29</strong> fixed the game-over freeze in Chapter 6 lobbies. The 1.0.21 phase adjustments described above still stand.</p>5:["category","bosses","d"]
