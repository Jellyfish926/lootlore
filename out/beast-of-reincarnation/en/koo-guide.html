Koo Commands & Skills, Explained

Now that Beast of Reincarnation has launched, Koo is exactly what Game Freak promised: not a pet gimmick, but the second half of the combat system. The "one-person, one-dog action RPG" runs the dog on a command menu that plays like a turn-based battle dropped into real-time, Sekiro-style parry combat. Here's how the whole loop actually works in the shipped game — plus the Rapport, exploration and growth systems the pre-launch coverage barely touched, and which numbers are still being verified.

The core loop: parry → Florescence Points → the △ command menu

Beast of Reincarnation splits its combat brain in two. Emma handles the real-time half — katana strings, dodges, and above all parries — while Koo handles the tactical half through a command menu. On PS5 you open that menu by pressing △ (Triangle). The important thing the shipped game makes clear is that opening it slows time to a crawl but does not pause the fight: Emma still has to walk, space and defend in real time while you read the board and pick Koo's move. The two halves are welded together by one resource — Florescence Points (FP), the currency that pays for everything the dog does. The loop works in three beats:

  1. Emma parries. A perfect block or parry with Emma's katana is the primary way to generate FP — a Sekiro-style deflect beats a dodge here. Landing regular attacks contributes too, but precise defense is the engine.
  2. FP banks into a shared pool. Defense literally converts into offense — "defence feeds offence" is the whole design in four words. There is no separate "dog mana" to manage; your parry game and Koo's output are the same economy.
  3. You open Koo's △ command menu. Time slows while the menu is open, giving you room to read the battlefield and pick a skill, exactly like choosing a move in a turn-based battle. Confirm, and Koo executes it in real time while Emma keeps fighting.

That tempo — turn-based decision-making grafted onto technical action — is Game Freak's Pokémon battle-design DNA showing through in its first big non-Pokémon action RPG. The stated intent is that you shouldn't need pure reflexes to survive: the slow-down window is deliberate breathing room for strategy, which is why the game earns its Sekiro comparisons on rhythm without being quite as punishing. Post-launch reviews back this up — the combat, with its parry economy, elemental options and multi-build skill trees, is consistently singled out as the game's strongest pillar.

Bloom Arts: what Koo's skills actually do

Koo's abilities all fall under the banner of Bloom Arts. In the shipped game they cover a wide spread of roles rather than pure damage:

  • Guard-breaks — arts that crack an enemy's defense to open a punish, useful for extending Emma's combos or capitalizing on a parry she just landed.
  • Crowd control — abilities that stagger or interrupt groups when Emma is being swarmed, buying space in a game with no co-op partner to peel for you.
  • Heal & support — Koo can patch Emma up and pull her out of a pinch, so his kit is a safety net as much as a damage source.
  • Gap-close — arts that drag the fight back into range so Emma can follow up.
  • Group damage with status ailments — wide-hitting arts that layer debuffs across a pack.

A few specific Bloom Art effects have been recorded in play — treat these as observed examples, not an official complete list:

  • An acid-type art that hits a group and applies a status ailment.
  • A giant-root art that binds and roots a target in place.
  • A seed-barrage art that adds hitstun and heals Emma while it fires.

The unlock order is now documented: Nushi Skills arrive boss by boss — Sakura Storm (Rangifer, Ch.1), Dokuzeri Shower (Lacerta, Ch.2), Chimeric Flow (Shidou & Nue, Ch.3), Charging Botan (Taurus, Ch.4) and Acid Burst Nobudou (Corvus, Ch.7), with art upgrades and Finishers from the late-game seals; the full reward map is on the boss guide. Exact FP costs are tuning values that shift with patches, so we point to in-game menus rather than freeze numbers here — full details coming soon.

The Down Gauge: Koo is your stagger engine

Every enemy carries two bars: a standard health bar and a secondary Down Gauge. Sustained pressure — Emma's hits and parries plus Koo's Bloom Arts — fills the gauge, and topping it out staggers the target: ordinary mobs open up to an execution, and bosses get stunned. That reframes what Koo is for. He isn't just bonus damage; he's the tempo tool you spend FP on at the exact moment a gauge is about to break, turning a stagger window into a finisher instead of a few ordinary hits. Against the giant Malefacts that anchor each region, gauge management matters as much as raw health damage.

Two different slow-time systems — don't confuse them

Koo's command menu isn't the only time-slow in the game, and mixing the two up is a common early mistake. Emma has her own: the Entanglement Gauge builds over seven stages and, when spent, triggers Entanglement Overdrive — a slow-time super that scales with the Strength attribute and can be upgraded across five ranks. Koo's △ menu is a tactical window you can open on demand to issue a command; Overdrive is Emma's charged ultimate. Both bend time, but they run on separate meters and do completely different things.

Tactical reads from the live game

Parry greed is the resource engine

Since perfect blocks and parries feed the FP pool, aggressive-but-precise defense out-earns passive dodging. The best players fish for parries deliberately, the way Sekiro players fish for deflects to break posture — except here the reward isn't just stagger, it's fuel for the dog. If Koo feels starved, the fix is almost always to parry more and dodge less.

The menu is free thinking time mid-boss

Time-slow while the △ command menu is open means every hairy moment — a second phase opening, a swarm spawn, a wind-up you don't recognize — is a chance to slow down, assess and answer. Build the habit of opening the menu when you're confused, not just when you're spending FP.

Spend on the gauge, not on cooldown

Because Koo is your stagger engine, the highest-value moment to fire a Bloom Art is when an enemy's Down Gauge is nearly full. Dumping FP into a guard-break or heavy art right before the gauge breaks converts it into an execution or boss stun rather than a handful of ordinary hits.

Rapport: petting Koo actually makes him stronger

One of the launch game's quieter systems is Rapport — Koo's bond with Emma. Petting him and interacting between fights raises Rapport, and higher Rapport translates into more combat power for Koo plus unlockable rewards. It isn't just flavor: time spent at rest with the dog feeds back into how hard he hits in the field, so it's worth doing rather than skipping. The exact Rapport tiers and the thresholds where each bonus kicks in are still being pinned down — coming soon.

Koo out of combat: scout, forager, and aggro magnet

Koo pulls his weight well outside boss arenas. In exploration he:

  • Barks a warning before ambushes, flagging enemies lying in wait before they spring — a genuine early-warning system worth listening for.
  • Points you toward hidden loot and rest points you'd otherwise walk straight past.
  • Digs up buried items when directed.
  • Pulls aggro, peeling enemies off Emma so you can reposition or set up a parry.

Get in the habit of watching the dog: following his nose is often how you find the map's off-path rewards, and his barking buys you the half-second you need to turn an ambush into a clean parry.

Koo's own growth line

Koo doesn't just ride Emma's coattails — he grows on his own track. The launch game gives him an independent progression path fed by a skill tree and SP, separate from Emma's trees, while equipment and inlaid Spirit Stones layer on top. Above all that, Nushi Skills — abilities absorbed after beating the region bosses — expand what the pair can do together, so Koo's ceiling rises on several tracks at once. The core build advice mirrors Emma's: concentrate your SP around the equipment effects you're leaning on rather than spreading thin. Precise level caps and the full shape of Koo's skill tree are still being mapped and will be added here — coming soon.

The one fight without Koo: Crimson Malefact

Because this entire guide is about leaning on the dog, it's worth flagging the exception the launch game throws at you. Roughly 18 hours in, a forced late-game boss called the Crimson Malefact waits at the top of Starfall Tower — and you fight it as Emma alone, with no Koo at your side. The whole parry-to-FP-to-Bloom-Arts loop is off the table for that encounter, so the second half of your combat kit simply isn't there. Shore up your Emma-only fundamentals — parry timing, dodges and katana pressure — before you climb the tower, and don't rely on a Bloom Art to bail you out of a bad read.

How Koo fits the four build directions

The launch game supports four broad build directions, and Koo's role shifts with each (our builds page breaks the stat and gear math down):

  • Melee suppression — constant parries mean the fastest FP income, so Bloom Arts come up almost on rotation and Koo plays like a second attacker.
  • Ranged elemental — bow and crossbow builds trade parry uptime for elemental pressure (arrows and bolts take up to three element slots — Fire, Lightning/Shock, Wet), so FP arrives slower and Koo leans toward control and setup between shots.
  • Stealth execution — you fish for openings and executions, and Koo's guard-breaks and aggro-pulls help engineer them.
  • Koo parry-flow (Bloom Arts) — a build that treats the dog as the main win condition: parry relentlessly to keep FP flowing and chain Bloom Arts as your core damage and control.

Whichever direction you pick, the through-line is the same: FP comes from defense, so the more you parry, the more Koo you get to play with.

Koo's skins: cosmetic only

Koo is also the face of the game's edition incentives, but none of his cosmetics touch the mechanics above — they're purely visual. The Digital Deluxe Edition ($69.99) adds a black-and-brown skin for Koo, on top of Emma's "Oni's Hat", the "Big Dipper" sword, 100,000 Amber, and vegetable seeds. The Standard Edition ($59.99) has all the same gameplay — the $10 gap is entirely appearance, items and in-game currency, so nothing in this guide changes between editions. Both run on PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC (Steam and Microsoft Store); on Xbox and PC the game is on Game Pass day one. Full breakdown on the editions page.

Who Koo is in the story (spoiler-light)

The setup, now that the game is out: Emma is a Sealer — an outcast shunned for her affliction, with vines for hair and a katana-and-bow kit — making her way across a post-apocalyptic Japan of the year 4026. Koo is the blighted canine that travels with her, and their bond is the emotional spine of the whole game precisely because it shouldn't work: she hunts blighted creatures, and he is one. The pair journey toward the source of the Blight — the Beast of Reincarnation itself. It's a deliberately melancholy, tear-jerking story about breaking a cycle, and launch reviews single out that bond as the heart of the game even where they're harder on the world around it. Koo FAQ

Can you control Koo directly?

Not fully. Koo is semi-controlled: you direct him through the △ command menu rather than piloting him like a second character. You choose which Bloom Art he uses and when, but Emma is the one you're actually moving in real time.

What button opens the command menu on PC and Xbox?

On PS5 it's △ (Triangle); on Xbox the equivalent face button (Y), and on PC the binding is rebindable in the options menu. Community control references such as Game8's controls list document the defaults per platform — we'd rather point you there and to the in-game menu than freeze a value that patches can chan guess.

Is there any point where I don't have Koo?

Yes. A forced late-game boss, the Crimson Malefact at the top of Starfall Tower (around the 18-hour mark), is an Emma-only fight with no Koo. It's the one place your Bloom Arts loop is off the table, so practice your parry fundamentals before you get there.

Does petting Koo actually do anything?

It does. Raising Rapport through petting and interaction increases Koo's combat power and unlocks rewards. The achievement funnel shows the climb: Man's Best Friend at 63.7% of players, Closest Companion 38.2%, Bestie 34.1%, and The Perfect Pal at just 6.6% — maxing the bond is a full-campaign commitment. Exact tier thresholds aren't surfaced by the game, so treat the achievements as the verified — coming soon.

Do I need Sekiro-level reflexes to keep Koo fed?

Less than you'd think. You do need to parry to earn FP, but the time-slow command menu is designed so decision-making — not raw twitch — carries fights. Perfect blocks beat dodging for FP, so leaning into defense is the efficient play.

How long is the adventure Koo stars in?

Roughly 30 hours for the main story, spread across about 13 chapters plus an epilogue — closer to a focused action RPG than an open-world epic. Completionist runs, which require New Game+, trend far longer.
