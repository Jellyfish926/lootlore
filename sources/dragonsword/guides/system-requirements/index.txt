3:T1d00,<p>DragonSword: Awakening is an Unreal Engine 5 open-world game in a 25 GB package, Windows only. The published requirements are moderate, but one line on the store page is doing more work than the rest of the table combined.</p>
<h2>Minimum</h2>
<ul><li>Requires a 64-bit processor and operating system</li><li><strong>OS:</strong> Windows 10</li><li><strong>Processor:</strong> Intel i5-9400F</li><li><strong>Memory:</strong> 8 GB RAM</li><li><strong>Graphics:</strong> NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660</li><li><strong>DirectX:</strong> Version 12</li><li><strong>Storage:</strong> 25 GB available space</li></ul>
<h2>Recommended</h2>
<ul><li>Requires a 64-bit processor and operating system</li><li><strong>OS:</strong> Windows 10</li><li><strong>Processor:</strong> Intel i7-9700F</li><li><strong>Memory:</strong> 16 GB RAM</li><li><strong>Graphics:</strong> NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060</li><li><strong>DirectX:</strong> Version 12</li><li><strong>Storage:</strong> 25 GB available space</li></ul>
<h2>The SSD line is not boilerplate</h2>
<p>Both tiers carry the same note, and Hound13 marked it with a warning symbol:</p>
<blockquote>※ IMPORTANT: An SSD is strictly required for an optimal gameplay experience. Installing the game on an HDD may result in extended loading times, texture pop-in, framerate drops, or <strong>unexpected crashes</strong>.</blockquote>
<p>Most publishers list "SSD recommended." This one lists crashes as a consequence. In an open-world UE5 title that streams assets as you move, that is a plausible failure mode rather than legal cover. If you have a spare HDD and a full SSD, this is not the game to compromise on.</p>

<h2>No macOS, no Linux build</h2>
<p>Windows only. There is no native Linux build — Steam Deck and desktop Linux run it through Proton, which Hound13 stated before launch.</p>
<h2>Steam Deck: Playable</h2>
<p>Valve rates it <strong>Playable</strong>, not Verified. The single flagged caveat is <strong>interface text is not legible</strong> — some in-game text is small on a 7-inch screen. Everything else passes: the default controller configuration is fully functional, controller glyphs match the Deck, and the default configuration is performant.</p>
<p>A community preset that reportedly holds <em>"very stable 30 FPS"</em>:</p>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Setting</th><th>Value</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Texture</td><td>High</td></tr><tr><td>FPS Limit</td><td>30</td></tr><tr><td>V-Sync</td><td>ON</td></tr><tr><td>Shadow</td><td>Normal</td></tr><tr><td>Depth of Field</td><td>ON</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Effect Quality</strong></td><td><strong>LOW</strong> — the biggest single performance lever</td></tr><tr><td>View Distance</td><td>Normal</td></tr><tr><td>Character Animation Optimization</td><td>ON</td></tr><tr><td>AMD FSR</td><td>High Quality</td></tr><tr><td>Frame Generation</td><td>OFF</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>Effect Quality is the setting to drop first on any hardware, not just the Deck.</p>
<h2>Two crashes with known workarounds</h2>
<p><strong>D3D12 crash on certain GPUs and drivers.</strong> Acknowledged by Hound13 as an open issue at the time of writing. Workaround: add <strong><code>-dx11</code></strong> to the game's launch options in Steam (right-click → Properties → Launch Options).</p>
<p><strong>Startup crash after changing certain graphics settings.</strong> Delete <code>DS\Saved\Config\Windows\GameUserSettings.ini</code> and the game will regenerate it with defaults. This is also the fix players use for the forced-upscaling complaint — one reviewer describes upscaling that could not be disabled and "messed up my screen" until the config file was deleted.</p>
<p>Frame Generation specifically has a history here: enabling it crashed the game in an early retail patch (fixed in 1.0.2), and it caused crashes in the demo builds too. If you are troubleshooting, turn it off first.</p>
<h2>Performance reports from players</h2>
<p>Two patterns show up consistently and are worth calibrating against.</p>
<p><strong>It runs well for its class.</strong> Optimisation is one of the most-praised aspects in reviews, including on Linux, integrated graphics and the Deck.</p>
<p><strong>Stutter can worsen with progress.</strong> One 24-hour review: <em>"Performance wise its fine if it wasn't for some random stutters that i get even while capped at 60fps… Edit: the performance starts tanking later in the game to the point of constant stutter."</em> This is a single attributed report, not a measured pattern, but it is a specific enough claim to keep in mind if your first few hours are smooth.</p>
<p>Invisible textures have their own discussion thread on the Steam board.</p>
<h2>Backing up your saves</h2>
<p>Hound13's stated policy is that mods and save editing are unsupported. Independently of that, multiple players have reported <strong>save loss</strong> — one lost 31 hours, another lost their save after quitting during a bugged quest, neither with mods involved.</p>
<p>Saves live in <code>DS\Saved\SaveGames\[SteamUserID]\</code>. Copy that folder somewhere periodically. It costs nothing and there is no in-game cloud rollback to fall back on.</p>
<h2>Price, size and what is in the box</h2>
<p><strong>$29.99</strong> at launch, no discount, no Early Access period — full release day one. A free demo ran 5 June to 7 July 2026 and was removed afterwards; demo saves did not carry over.</p>
<p>The base game includes 19 heroes, 8 chapters, 59 Steam achievements, Steam Cloud, family sharing, and co-op via invite codes (2-player Battle, 3-player Raid). Four additional heroes are on the roadmap as <strong>free</strong> content, not DLC.</p>
<p><strong>Eleven languages</strong> for interface and subtitles: English, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, French, German, Spanish (Spain), Russian, Thai and Portuguese (Brazil). <strong>Korean is the only language with full audio.</strong></p>
<h2>About the DLC</h2>
<p>There are 20 DLC items — a $19.99 Deluxe Pack (familiar, artbook, OST), costumes at $3.99, swimsuit costumes at $7.99, and familiars at $3.99.</p>
<p>One point deserves flagging on a specs page because it affects the buying decision rather than performance: <strong>familiars are not purely cosmetic.</strong> They come in types, and owning a complete type set grants movement bonuses — per-movement-type speed and stamina-cost reduction. Every type has one or two familiars locked behind paid DLC. This is the most-cited criticism in the game's negative reviews, and the top-voted one cites <em>"nearly $80 of DLC on the steam page."</em></p>
