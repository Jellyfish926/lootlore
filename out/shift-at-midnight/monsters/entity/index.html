    Every other threat in the game arrives on a schedule. This one arrives because of you. Wave a doppelganger through the counter and it comes back that same shift, in its real form, to hunt you — and on blood-rain nights it comes whether you made a mistake or not.

    The loop this page sits inside

    The Entity is the second half of a mistake whose first half happened at the counter. Something walked in wearing a stolen face, its paperwork held up, you rang the purchase through — and the moment it cleared the door, tonight stopped being a quiet shift. That is the whole design: identification is the test, the Hunt is the grade.

    Which means the best Entity strategy is not on this page at all. It is the database search you did not run an hour earlier. Everything below is damage control.

    How a Hunt actually plays

    The Hunt comes at the end of the shift, not the moment the doppelganger escapes — you will usually know it is coming and have time to spend money on it. That window is the difference between the two achievement numbers up top: nearly everyone survives a hunt they prepared for, fewer than half have ever ended one quickly.

    • Spend the warning. Ammunition, a second weapon, board placement — the shop is open and the threat is scheduled. Walking into a Hunt with shift earnings unspent is the classic error.
    • Barricade before it arrives, not during. Wooden boards on doors and windows slow it down; hammering them while something blind is navigating by sound is self-defeating.
    • Decide in advance who shoots. In co-op, one armed player holding a chokepoint beats three people firing at noises — every shot is also a signal flare. See the survival guide for trap placement.

    It is blind. Behave accordingly

    The Entity does not see you. It hears you — footsteps, doors, gunfire, and proximity voice chat. The game routes your actual microphone through the world, so a teammate narrating the monster’s position is, mechanically, bait.

    This inverts normal horror-game instincts. Line of sight does not matter; a lit room is not more dangerous than a dark one; standing still and silent in the open can be safer than rustling behind cover. The discipline is auditory: stop moving when it is near, mute the panic, and let it walk past.

    The “five types” claim

    One outlet describes five distinct Entity forms with different behaviours. No second source confirms a count, the developer has not published one, and the patch notes never mention types. We are treating the number as unverified and not building a taxonomy on it — if the developer documents the forms, this page will.

    Blood Moon nights — the blood rain

    Every few shifts the game hangs a red sky over the station: blood rain falls, and the Hunt at the end of that shift is guaranteed — no counter mistake required. The reviewer phrasing is exact: you load in with “the ominous warning that the entity is guaranteed to strike at the end of the shift.”

    That changes the night’s budget, not just its mood. On a normal shift, perfect ID work buys you a quiet close; on a Blood Moon it buys you nothing at the door and everything before it — the shift is a countdown to a fight you already know is booked, so board placement and ammunition stop being contingency spending and become the plan.

    Community shift-tables place the first Blood Moon around Shift 5 and a second around Shift 10, with the later one making weapons harder to reach mid-fight. Both placements come from player documentation rather than the developer, so treat the exact numbers as reported, not promised — the procedural generation this game runs on has broken schedules before.

    Blood rain: TheXboxHub. Shift placement: community shift tables, unconfirmed by the developer.

    Killing it versus outlasting it

    Both are legitimate. The Entity dies to concentrated fire, and Relentless — finish a hunt within 30 seconds — sits at 45.4%, so nearly half the player base has managed an efficient kill at least once. But the achievement for merely surviving sits at 93.8%, and that gap is honest information: the monster is beatable, and it is also entirely optional to beat. A barricaded, silent crew that lets the clock run has lost nothing.

    Pick based on the night, not on pride. Ammunition already bought, group together, doors held — shoot. Scattered, broke, or down a player — go quiet and give it nothing to hear.

    What it is not

    • It is not the Dentist. The final shift’s pursuer cannot be fought at all. The Entity always can.
    • It is not the Shrieking Doll, though the two arrive in the same breath — the Doll is the fragile escort, and shooting it at the wrong moment is a sound decision in the worst sense. One bullet, chosen carefully.
    • It is not a Rake — Rakes are the endless-mode predators added on 29 July, and they hunt your customers, not you. See the bestiary for that distinction.

    What we are not publishing

    Health values, damage numbers, movement speed, hearing radius, or a type-by-type behaviour table. None of that has a source we can stand behind — the developer publishes none of it, and the one outlet counting five forms is alone in doing so. What is on this page is the shape of the encounter, which is the part that decides whether you live.
