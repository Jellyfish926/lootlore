    Shift At Midnight survival guide

    Last verified 5 August 2026 · game version: 29 July 2026 patch

    Surviving a hunt is mostly about sound and doors, and only lastly about shooting. Weapons matter — they are just the fourth thing that matters.

    The exception, stated first

    None of this works on the Dentist, who arrives on Shift 13: immune to your weapons, and traps do not stop him. The only published route through that encounter is to run at Sheriff Clyde without hiding and without looking back, until the cutscene takes over. Single source, not independently confirmed.

    Hunts are something you caused

    They are not weather. Let a doppelganger complete its purchase and walk out and it returns that same night as a monster (Game Rant), so everything below is the bill for a decision made at the counter — see identifying doppelgangers. Still Breathing, for surviving your first hunt, sits at 93.8%; Relentless, for finishing one inside 30 seconds, sits at 45.4%. That gap is this page’s subject: surviving is normal, ending it fast is a plan.

    Sound is the first thing to control

    Noise gives away your position, so silence is a defensive tool before any barricade is — and a gun is the loudest thing you own, which is why a Shrieking Doll shot at the wrong moment can cost more than it saves. Both points are single-sourced and not independently confirmed. Sound works for you too: since the 23 July patch the Jack-in-the-Box is much louder, turning the search for it into a listening problem, and in Endless Mode a screaming customer means a Rake has spawned.

    Barricades, traps and hiding

    Barricades buy time and shape movement. The station is small with few ways through it, so the right door does not just delay a threat — it forces it onto a path you chose. Barricading reactively into a room with one exit converts a chase into a corner: block to redirect, not to hide behind.

    Traps are prediction: they pay off where a threat has to go, not where it happens to be, so the value comes from knowing the chokepoints before the night turns bad. Laying them mid-chase is useless. They are also the only published answer to a Demented, which cannot be shot down.

    Hiding is a reset, not a solution: it breaks contact so you can get back to a shift that is still running. The quota does not pause because you are under a counter.

    They do not all want the same thing

    • Entities — the default hunters, and what you get for letting a doppelganger leave. Barricades, traps and weapons all work; they get harder as the run goes on.
    • Shrieking Doll — small, crawls low, finds you by line of sight, dies to a few shots. An interruption rather than a threat, but a noisy one to remove. Single source.
    • Demented — cannot move while you look straight at it. Hold the stare, back it toward a trap, then break eye contact. Not rare, whatever you have read: 79.8% of players have killed one.
    • Marionette — from Shift 9 onward, flagged in advance by a N.E.T. email. When the music box starts, find it and hold E to rewind before the melody plays three times; it appears in the break room, a storage room, the bathroom or a shelf aisle. It can be killed, and the 23 July HP cut makes that a real option with a stocked arsenal and a second player.
    • Rakes — Endless and post-story only. They come out of the forest and go for your customers rather than you: follow the screaming, look for red light at the treeline, kill it before it reaches the building. Beyond “they exist and emerge from the forests”, single-sourced. See Endless Mode.
    • The Dentist — see the top of this page.
    Learn the layout on a quiet night

    Every skill here depends on knowing the building: which aisles connect, where the loops are, which corners are dead ends. Map knowledge is the one asset that works against everything, including the entity you cannot fight.

    Everything above is about not needing to shoot. The rest is what you can buy for when you do.

    Weapons: what you can buy and what it is for

    Achievement: Locked And Loaded 23.5% of players

    Melee is the achievement; the guns are insurance

    Locked And Loaded is specific: purchase all melee weapons and fill out the weapons arsenal. At 23.5% it is a budgeting problem, not a difficulty one: restocking pays tonight, the arsenal pays on a night that may never come, and under pressure people buy the immediate thing. Decide early that a fixed slice of each shift’s takings is untouchable.

    Firearms sit outside that achievement, and there are now two of them: the 29 July patch added a second purchasable gun alongside the one the game shipped with. They are also the loudest tools you own, which is the argument for melee on a night you would rather not be found — see sound discipline above. Full change list: patch notes.

    Have it equipped before the hunt

    Buy and equip before a hunt starts, not during one: Relentless — finish a hunt within 30 seconds, 45.4% — is close to impossible if the first ten seconds go on shopping. The target to attempt it on is a Shrieking Doll, which comes to you rather than hiding. Never on a Dentist night, which cannot be won at all.

    What a weapon does not solve

    • The Dentist. No weapon works. Running is the entire answer.
    • Doppelgangers. The problem is identification, not damage — a weapon applied to the wrong customer is the 96.9% achievement. See identifying doppelgangers.
    • An unwound music box. Whether you fight a Marionette at all is settled by the box, not your loadout. A weapon helps once the fight starts — the 23 July patch cut its HP — but arriving armed does not substitute for winding.

    We do not publish a weapon tier list, damage values or per-monster recommendations. There is no published list of the melee weapons or their prices, and the confident numbers circulating for this game are unsourced.
