3:T1bea,<p>Survival is the biggest raw health line among the seven <a href="/guides/talents/">talent</a> trees and the only one whose capstone is a net loss on the stat it spends the rest of the tree building. Two points of maximum health per point spent, forty at the cap — and then a level 20 that removes twelve inventory slots in exchange for a system.</p>

<h2>What the three breakpoints do</h2>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Points spent</th><th>What arrives</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>5</td><td>Drinking any potion also grants one random stat</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td>Three more inventory slots</td></tr><tr><td>20</td><td>Twelve inventory slots removed; Tablet Imprinting unlocked</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h2>Points 1–10 and points 11–20 are two different talents</h2>
<p>Up to ten, this tree is the straightforward beginner purchase the <a href="/guides/beginner/">beginner guide</a> describes: flat health, plus three cells. Past ten, you are buying nothing except a countdown to a penalty. There is no intermediate breakpoint between 10 and 20, so every point in that band is two maximum health and a step closer to losing a third of your board.</p>
<p>The board is the reason that trade is severe rather than merely expensive. The <a href="/guides/inventory-grid/">inventory grid</a> page documents it precisely: the grid is six columns wide, starts at 6×4 = 24 cells, and the Destiny Inscription's <em>Expanding Pouch</em> node adds six across three ranks to reach 6×5 = 30. Twelve cells off a full thirty is two complete rows. The <a href="/tablets/tablet-layout-examples/">tablet layout examples</a> page counts the same cost in layouts rather than cells — the penalty is "two of these layouts gone."</p>
<h2>Every slot source, in one place</h2>
<p>Survival 10's three cells are one of a short list, and it is worth seeing the whole list before spending ten points on it:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Expanding Pouch</strong> in the <a href="/guides/destiny-inscription/">Destiny Inscription</a> — +6, one full row, across three ranks</li><li><strong>Ingenuity 5</strong> — +2</li><li><strong>Survival 10</strong> — +3</li><li><strong>The Explorer Miracle</strong> — +6, but it grants no starting item</li><li><strong>Magic Workshop</strong> — +2, reserved for potions only</li><li>In-run inventory expansion event rooms</li></ul>
<p>Ingenuity's two slots arrive five points into a tree whose capstone is a universal damage multiplier; Survival's three arrive ten points into a tree whose capstone removes twelve. Per point, and per what the rest of the tree is worth to you, those two purchases are not close.</p>
<h2>What Tablet Imprinting actually buys</h2>
<p>Imprinting consumes a tablet and engraves its effect permanently into the bag, freeing the cell the tablet occupied. That is the counter-argument to the twelve-slot bill: a board with enough engraved effects needs fewer physical tablets on it, so the usable space is not the raw cell count. The <a href="/guides/talents/">talents page</a> quotes a player who has run it calling it useful for getting a first god run going — note which run, and that the phrase is theirs rather than ours.</p>
<p>Two 1.0.30 fixes changed how safe the system is to use. The <a href="/patch-notes/1-0-25-to-1-0-30/">1.0.25–1.0.30 patch page</a> records that Tablet Imprinting no longer consumes the tablet when activation conditions fail, and that undiscardable tablets can no longer be fed into the Tablet Combiner. The <a href="/tablets/tablet-solver-and-placement/">tablet solver page</a> makes the practical point that follows: planning tools work on cell numbers and cannot see either fix, so plan the layout in a solver and confirm the imprint in the game.</p>
<h2>The level 5 line is the one nobody discusses</h2>
<p>One random stat per potion drunk is the smallest breakpoint in the tree and the one most sensitive to how you play. Its value is a function of how many potions you actually consume in a run, and the repo's potion-side facts point in two directions. The <a href="/guides/destiny-inscription/">Destiny Inscription</a> offers <em>Magic Workshop</em>, which adds two potion-only slots, and <em>Rylie's Gift</em>, which starts every run with a free Restoration Potion — both push the number of potions up. <a href="/guides/hard-mode/">Hard Mode</a>'s <em>Tension</em> modifier, on the other hand, blocks potions during boss combat for a single tier, which removes the effect from precisely the fights where an extra stat would matter.</p>
<p>Which stat is granted, and whether the roll can repeat, is <strong>not stated</strong> in any source in this repo. Treat the line as "a small random bonus per potion" and no more precise than that.</p>
<h2>Reading old guides about this tree</h2>
<p>Per the <a href="/guides/system-requirements/">system requirements</a> page, the talent that grants Tablet Imprinting has appeared as <em>Survive</em>, <em>Endurance</em> and <em>Survival</em> across different translation passes of the patch notes. A pre-1.0 guide recommending "Endurance" for health and slots is talking about this tree, not the defence tree, regardless of what your current screen calls it.</p>
<h2>Where this leaves the spread</h2>
<p>The standing reading across this site is consistent: Survival is a first-clear purchase up to 10 and an endgame purchase at 20, with nothing sensible in between. If you are considering the capstone, put the spread into the <a href="/guides/talent-planner/">talent planner</a> first — it prints the inventory penalty in red rather than burying it in a table row, which is the difference between deciding to pay twelve slots and discovering it mid-run.</p>
