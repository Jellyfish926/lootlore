3:T22a6,<p>Your first run of Sephiria will end on floor 1 or 2, and that is fine — it is supposed to. The thing worth knowing before you start is that <strong>you are not playing to win the run, you are playing to bank Sapphires</strong>, because Sapphires buy the Destiny Inscription nodes that make later runs winnable. You earn them <em>on death</em>, scaled to how deep you got and how many <a href="/bosses/chapters-and-bosses/">bosses</a> you killed. Dying productively is a real strategy here.</p>
<figure class="fig"><img src="/images/sephiria-route-node-map-1200.webp" srcset="/images/sephiria-route-node-map-800.webp 800w, /images/sephiria-route-node-map-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 720px" alt="Sephiria route node map showing Mole Stronghold and a Money event choice" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Each floor branches into node choices; events preview their reward before you commit. <h2>What a run is actually made of</h2>
<p>Each floor has the same skeleton: a <strong>Starting Room</strong>, then a <strong>choice of 3 nodes</strong>, then a <strong>Boss Room</strong>. Every starting room except floor 1's has a fixed shop and a training dummy you can hit to check your damage.</p>
<p>The node icons carry two pieces of information. The <strong>top</strong> tells you the reward type:</p>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Node</th><th>What it gives</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Level Up</td><td>XP and a stat/artifact reward</td></tr><tr><td>Artifact</td><td>an artifact choice</td></tr><tr><td>Tablet</td><td>a tablet choice</td></tr><tr><td>Enchantment</td><td>an altar that raises an artifact's level 1–3 times</td></tr><tr><td>Anvil</td><td>a weapon upgrade</td></tr><tr><td>Dice</td><td>rerolls (only after you unlock dice)</td></tr><tr><td>Green Heart</td><td>+15 Max HP</td></tr><tr><td>Money</td><td>Leaf, the in-run currency</td></tr><tr><td>Shop</td><td>a merchant</td></tr><tr><td>Tree Roots</td><td>one Miracle — appears once per run</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>The <strong>bottom</strong> tells you the risk: plain <strong>Common</strong>, <strong>Derangement</strong> (double-knife icon — enemies are randomly enhanced), or <strong>Mini Boss</strong> (grey skull — the floor's mini-boss is added, and it drops an artifact).</p>
<p>Random events do not show on the map at all. You find them by walking into them.</p>
<h2>The five things new players get wrong</h2>
<p><strong>1. Treating the bag as storage.</strong> <a href="/artifacts/artifacts-and-tablets/">Artifacts</a> sit in a grid, and <a href="/tablets/all-tablets/">Tablets</a> placed in that grid raise the <em>level</em> of whatever artifact is in each affected cell. A maxed artifact and an unmaxed one are not close in value. Three well-placed items beat eight scattered ones.</p>
<p><strong>2. Skipping anvils.</strong> Weapon upgrades gate on stage progression — skip them early and later anvils can stop appearing. Take every one you see.</p>
<p><strong>3. Spreading across combos.</strong> Artifacts carry tags (#Ember, #Glacier, #Companion, #Magitech, #Shadow…) and the bonus fires at a <em>count</em> threshold, usually 2 or 3 items. Two tags at threshold beat six tags at one item each.</p>
<p><strong>4. Not opening the Journal.</strong> Press Esc → Journal. It shows every artifact you have seen and the full upgrade path for every weapon, so you can plan a route instead of improvising at the anvil.</p>
<p><strong>5. Not using the Training Grounds.</strong> The Comprehensive Training Grounds, added in 0.11.5, has an anvil next to the guard NPC. You can try a weapon's whole upgrade tree there without spending a run on it.</p>
<h2>The unlock order that actually helps</h2>
<p>Destiny Inscription is the tree you reach by talking to the Tree. Nodes cost Sapphires, and full completion costs <strong>383</strong> — so you are prioritising for a long time. A sane order:</p>
<ol><li><strong>Tablet</strong> (free) — turns tablets on at all. Nothing works without this.</li><li><strong>Home Repairs</strong> — unlocks Talent Point assignment, and gets you the <em>DIY</em> achievement. Tier-3 nodes need it.</li><li><strong>Talent Points</strong> — five nodes granting +1 through +5, 25 points total.</li><li><strong>Expanding Pouch</strong> — three ranks, +6 inventory slots, which is exactly one extra row.</li><li><strong>A second weapon</strong> — Heavy Weapon Training gets you the Great Sword. Buy the node, then complete the training quest with <strong>Moro</strong> in the village; buying alone does not unlock it.</li><li><strong>Seed Money</strong> — start runs with 200, then 400 Leaf.</li><li><strong>Dice</strong> — three nodes, up to 6 starting dice, plus <em>Dice Acquisition</em> (+2 on boss kill).</li></ol>
<p>Everything else can wait. Note that the top of the tree is currently blank — players with every purchasable node maxed report the counter reading <strong>99%</strong>, so do not chase 100%.</p>
<h2>Talents: pick two, not seven</h2>
<p>There are seven talents and you get 25 points from the tree (45 total if you later grind Hard Mode). They respec free at any time outside the dungeon, so experiment.</p>
<p>For a first clear, <strong>Endure</strong> and <strong>Survival</strong> carry hardest. Endure at level 5 hands you a Foundation tablet at the start of every run — a free +1 to a whole row — and at level 20 revives you once at 60% HP. Survival gives flat Max HP and, at level 10, +3 inventory slots.</p>
<p>Two traps worth flagging. <strong>Survival level 20 costs you 12 inventory slots</strong> in exchange for unlocking Tablet Imprinting; that is a late-game trade, not a beginner one. And <strong>Ingenuity level 20</strong> grants +7% damage per active combo type, which is enormous — but only once you have enough artifacts to run many combos at once.</p>
<h2>Events worth knowing before you meet them</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Lucky Shrine</strong> — pick +6 Max HP or +4 DEF, permanently. Common, take it.</li><li><strong>Blood Donation</strong> — pay 33% of current HP to the Iron Maiden for an item. A bat NPC is always present; killing it gives you Oath of the Vampire Lord but disables the Iron Maiden and tanks your Barter. Doing this five times unlocks the Wingless Bat costume.</li><li><strong>Dimensional Rift</strong> — spend Sapphires to permanently unlock artifacts into your pool. Eight artifacts and one tablet come from here and nowhere else.</li><li><strong>Wandering Witch</strong> — very rare, sells six items that are exclusive to her, with very low Barter so prices are cheap. Eleven artifacts and two tablets are only obtainable here.</li><li><strong>Double Doors</strong> — floor 3 only. 500 Leaf or 4 Sapphires for a random reward.</li><li><strong>Secret rooms</strong> — look for <strong>cracked walls</strong>, often high up, and hit them. They exist on floors 1–5 but not in Qliphoth's Temple. There is one on floor 3, bottom-left of the map after the Erma fight, with no visual hint at all.</li></ul>
<h2>What to expect from the first four bosses</h2>
<p>Floor 1 is <strong>Mogdi, the Black Hand</strong>, the mole chief — 89.7% of all Steam players have beaten him. Floor 2 is <strong>Rataka</strong>, the pillager leader. Floor 3 is <strong>Erma, the Mad Scientist</strong>. Floor 4 in Chapter 1 is <strong>Askard, the Cult Leader</strong>.</p>
<p>That last one matters for achievements: <em>Enlightened Scholar</em> requires killing Askard <strong>during Chapter 1</strong>, before he mutates. If you push into Chapter 2 first you will be fighting Mutated Askard instead and the achievement is gone for that save's progression window.</p>
<p>We deliberately do not publish boss attack patterns. A wrong tell sends you into a fight prepared for the wrong thing, and nothing in the public record documents movesets reliably.</p>
<h2>The honest expectation</h2>
<p>The store lists 6 chapters, 60+ enemy types and 10+ unique bosses. Steam's global achievement stats say <strong>14.5%</strong> of players have finished the game. That is not a difficulty warning so much as a shape: Sephiria expects many runs, and the meta-progression is generous enough that "I died on floor 3 again" and "I made progress" are usually the same sentence.</p>
