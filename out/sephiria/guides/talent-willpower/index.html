    Sephiria Willpower Talent: Luck and the Wish Fountain

    By Jellyfi · Published 2026-09-19 · Last reviewed 2026-09-19 · Game version checked 1.0.30

    Willpower

    Quick facts

    Per point
    +1 Luck per point (+20 Luck at 20 points)
    Notes
    No source states what Luck (the per-point stat) actually converts into -- drop rates, rarity weights, event outcomes, or a formula., Three of five documented builds that carry items via the Wish Fountain put 20 points in Willpower specifically for the extra carry-in capacity (see mechanic-wish-fountain).

    Willpower is one of two talent trees with an unlock requirement of its own — the talents page records that assignment opens with the Home Repairs quest, that Willpower additionally needs Terrain Recovery, and that Ingenuity has its own Destiny Inscription quest. It is also the only tree whose headline number this site refuses to state as a single figure. Both facts are worth understanding before you spend twenty points here.

    Getting the tree at all

    The Destiny Inscription page lists Terrain Recovery as the node that opens the Willpower tree, described there as the Luck line, and notes that it involves a hole in the Ark and a puzzle rather than a straight purchase. The glossary confirms the same location from the other direction: the Ark is where the puzzle that unlocks this tree lives. If the tree is greyed out on your screen, that errand is the missing step — the same structural gotcha as the Home Repairs quest that gates talent assignment in the first place.

    The breakpoints

    Points spentWhat arrives
    5Two extra starting dice, and Wish Fountain capacity +1
    10+15 maximum health and +25 maximum MP
    20A further Wish Fountain capacity increase — the figure is disputed

    The dispute, stated plainly

    The artifacts and tablets page quotes the 0.10.0 announcement verbatim: base Wish Fountain capacity rose from 3 to 4, and the maximum obtainable through the Willpower talent was reduced from 6 to 5. The same announcement then lists the level 5 effect as capacity +1 and the level 20 effect as an increase reduced from 6 to 4. Four plus one plus four is nine, or a talent contribution of five on a base of four — which is not a maximum of five however you read it.

    A third figure exists in the repo. The Destiny Inscription page describes the Wishing Fountain node as base capacity 4, extendable to 8 via this talent. That is consistent with neither of the two readings above.

    This site's position, stated on the common misconceptions page and repeated on the Wish Fountain page, is that the maximum is unconfirmed: the per-level lines are printed as published, the stated maximum contradicts them, and nothing in the 1.0 patch series has re-confirmed either. The talent planner reproduces the dispute on the breakpoint list rather than picking a number. Do not build a run around the exact fountain ceiling; build it around "twenty points here gives you meaningfully more carry-in capacity, and count the slots on your own screen."

    What the tree is actually bought for

    The Wish Fountain page makes the case better than the capacity number does: three of the five documented builds it catalogues put twenty points here, and they do it for carry-in capacity alone. Capacity is measured in artifacts rather than rarity, so a legendary costs the same slot as a common — which is why the carry-in lists on that page are short and almost entirely build-critical pieces.

    Two of those spreads are worth naming. The tier 50 evasion tank runs twenty points here doing double duty — luck on item rolls, plus the fountain capacity that lets it carry two artifacts in, the Crown of Humility and the Frostium Ring. The sword & shield page's Blizzard Hammer route is blunter still: the whole build is two rare-grade artifacts, and twenty points exist so both can be carried into the next run. That guide, written in the 0.7 era, calls the mechanism the "dimension pouch," which is the fountain's pre-rename form as far as this site can tell.

    That is the clearest statement of what this tree does. On a build whose core is two or three specific items, Willpower converts "find them again" into "start with them."

    Luck, and what nobody has published

    The per-point line is Luck, one point per talent point. No source in this repo states what Luck converts into — drop rates, rarity weights, event outcomes or something else — or gives any formula. The Wish Fountain page describes the tier 50 spread's use of it as luck on item rolls, which is the most specific description available and is an attributed reading rather than a documented mechanic. Treat "20 Luck means X% better drops" as unconfirmed wherever you see it.

    The level 5 dice are more concrete, and they stack onto an existing economy. The Destiny Inscription sells up to six starting dice across three nodes, plus Dice Acquisition for two more on a boss kill and Dice Transformation for one when you skip a reward. Two more at the start of every run is a reroll or two before the first floor has finished, which is when rerolls are worth most because the bag is still shapeable.

    The level 10 line is the quiet one

    Fifteen health and twenty-five MP for reaching ten is the only breakpoint in this tree that has nothing to do with luck or carry-in. On a caster that is a real MP pool increase, and it is the reason a build can justify stopping at ten here rather than committing the full twenty — you get the dice, the first capacity point and a stat block, without buying into a capstone whose size the official record cannot agree on.
