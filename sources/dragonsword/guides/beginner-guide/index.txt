<p>First, a correction that will save you an hour of confusion: <strong>DragonSword: Awakening is not a roguelite.</strong> It is a single persistent open world — the Continent of Orbis — with a chaptered main story. There are no runs, no procedural stages, and nothing resets. If you arrived expecting a run-based game, that expectation will fight you.</p>
<p>What it is: an anime-style open-world action RPG in Unreal Engine 5, built around <strong>tag-team combat driven by status ailments</strong>, with <strong>8 chapters</strong> and a main story the producer estimated at 10–15 hours (about 20 to complete all eight).</p>

<h2>The one system to learn first</h2>
<p>You field <strong>three heroes</strong> and control one at a time. Each has <strong>2 Active Skills and 2 Signal Skills</strong> — twelve skills in play across the team.</p>
<p>The loop: <strong>apply a status ailment → a Signal Skill arms → firing it switches heroes → the incoming hero applies theirs → repeat.</strong></p>
<p>Signal Skills have no cooldown; they gate on ailments instead. If the responder is off-field, an icon appears on their portrait and switching to them fires the skill as they tag in.</p>
<p>Two consequences that are not obvious:</p>
<p><strong>Basic attacks are not filler.</strong> They do little damage but chip skill cooldowns and build ailment stacks. Weaving them is the intended rhythm.</p>
<p><strong>Your team needs overlapping ailments, not complementary damage.</strong> Three heroes who each apply an ailment nobody else responds to will never chain. Read each hero's skill text for their trigger — the game does not show you a compatibility chart. Our <a href="/guides/status-ailments-and-signals/">combat page</a> covers this in detail.</p>

<h2>What to do about heroes early</h2>
<p>Eight of the nineteen heroes join through the story. The other eleven are bought in the <strong>Exchange</strong> with <strong>Fate's Invitation</strong> tickets, and there is no other source — no gacha, no paid characters. The game gives out enough invitations to eventually get everyone, so an early purchase is not a permanent loss.</p>
<p>Still, spend the first few on ailment coverage rather than on whoever looks strongest. <strong>Lute is the only hero with an on-demand heal</strong>, so he stays in most teams by default; build the other two slots around a shared ailment.</p>
<h2>Healing, and why cooking matters immediately</h2>
<p>There is <strong>no natural HP regeneration.</strong> Out of combat you heal at a Goddess Statue of Organa. In combat, food is your healing, and you can only equip <strong>two consumables at a time</strong>.</p>
<p>So <a href="/guides/cooking/">cooking</a> is not a side activity — it is the healing economy. Three methods: <strong>Grill</strong> restores HP or stamina, <strong>Boil</strong> gives buffs and some revival teas, <strong>Slice</strong> cuts stamina cost while climbing, swimming, dashing and flying. Recipes are discovered by experimenting, and three 4-star ingredients make a 5-star dish.</p>
<p>Patch 1.0.7 helped a lot here: statue usage is now <strong>unlimited</strong>, and dungeon entry and wipe recovery restore you to <strong>100% HP with the party revived</strong>. If you read older complaints about respawning at 20% health, those are outdated.</p>
<h2>What you will actually be doing</h2>
<p>Beyond the main story ("Adventure Quests," numbered to at least #59), the content list is long: Hero Quests, Bond Quests, Area Quests, Sudden Missions, Hunt dungeons, Trait dungeons, daily-resetting World <a href="/guides/bosses/">Bosses</a>, 3-player Raids, the Tower of Trials, Trial of Mist jumping puzzles, 29 sliding-pillar puzzles, pressure-plate puzzles, treasure maps, waypoints, and collectibles.</p>
<figure class="fig"><img src="/images/dragonsword-deep-sea-exploration-1200.webp" srcset="/images/dragonsword-deep-sea-exploration-800.webp 800w, /images/dragonsword-deep-sea-exploration-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 720px" alt="A lone hero swimming through a dim teal underwater area lit by shafts of light" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Orbis is one persistent open world — meadows, caves, and the treasure-filled deep seas. Nothing resets between sessions. Official screenshot — DragonSword: Awakening, Hound13 Inc.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Progression layers on top: hero levels, <strong>Mercenary Corps</strong> promotion quests that raise the level cap as story beats, <strong>Awakening</strong> tiers, <strong>Karma</strong> and Exclusive Karma, Master Skills, gear with substats, <strong>Runes</strong> with synthesis, and Familiar set bonuses.</p>
<p>That is a lot of systems, and reviewers do call the material sprawl excessive. Do not try to engage with all of it in your first ten hours.</p>
<h2>Co-op, briefly</h2>
<p>Co-op runs on <strong>invite codes</strong> and only in specific content: <strong>Battle supports 2 players, Raid supports 3.</strong> There is no open-world co-op and no player trading. The producer said the 2-player subjugation cap was deliberate — <em>"with 3 or more players, normal boss combat became too chaotic"</em> — and that raids were scaled down from an original 8-player design. Co-op is host-based, so there are no regional restrictions.</p>
<h2>The known issues to patch around</h2>
<p>Three are worth knowing before you start:</p>
<p><strong>D3D12 crashes on certain GPUs and drivers.</strong> Hound13 has acknowledged this as an open issue with a workaround: add <strong><code>-dx11</code></strong> to the game's launch options. This is the single most useful thing to know if the game will not start.</p>
<p><strong>A startup crash after certain graphics settings.</strong> The fix is deleting <code>DS\Saved\Config\Windows\GameUserSettings.ini</code>. Several players also report forced upscaling they could not disable without this.</p>
<p><strong>Save loss.</strong> Multiple users report saves vanishing — one lost 31 hours, another after quitting during a bugged quest. No mods were involved in either case. <strong>Back up <code>DS\Saved\SaveGames\[SteamUserID]\</code></strong> periodically. Hound13's stated policy is that mods and save editing are unsupported.</p>
<p>A fourth, if you are on Linux or Steam Deck: a "file tampering" error blocking lobby <em>creation</em> (joining still works) has been reported across Bazzite, Fedora and openSUSE, with one user resolving it only through a full reinstall.</p>
<h2>What to expect from the story</h2>
<p>An honest note, because it affects whether you buy: <strong>the main plot is not resolved when the credits roll.</strong> Multiple reviewers describe a cliffhanger ending and at least one unfinished questline, attributing it to the game's origin as a gacha title designed for continuous updates. Hound13 has a roadmap with four free heroes and further patches, but there is no guarantee of a story conclusion on any particular schedule.</p>
<p>The reception is nonetheless strongly positive — roughly 90% across 11,700+ reviews as of 1 September 2026, with praise concentrated on the combat feel, art and optimisation, and criticism concentrated on pacing, cutscene density and DLC structure.</p>
<h2>Fifty-nine achievements</h2>
<p>There are <strong>60 Steam achievements</strong> (1.0.9 added the sixtieth), 17 of them hidden. The most-unlocked is <em>Lucky!</em> at 45.9% (read 1 September 2026), which gives you a sense of how early most players stop. If you are achievement-hunting, note that several tie to content types you might otherwise skip entirely — Sudden Missions, Hunt monsters, Eona's Legacy collectibles and dungeon gates each have one.</p>5:["category","guides","d"]
