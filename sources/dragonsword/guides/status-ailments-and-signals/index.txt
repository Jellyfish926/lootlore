3:T20da,<p><em>Game version checked: 1.0.10 (19 August 2026 patch). DragonSword patches frequently — if a newer version is live, cross-check tuning-sensitive numbers in-game.</em></p>
<p>Hound13's own description of the system is unusually precise: <em>"Each of the 19 <a href="/heroes/all-heroes/">Heroes</a> possesses distinct Status Ailment abilities. Stack Status Ailments with Active Skills, then chain devastating combos with powerful Signal Skills."</em></p>
<p>Read that as a loop rather than a feature list, because that is what it is:</p>
<p><strong>Apply an ailment → a Signal Skill arms → firing it switches heroes → the incoming hero applies their ailment → repeat.</strong></p>
<p>Everything else in DragonSword's combat is in service of keeping that loop running.</p>
<h2>The nine ailments</h2>
<p>Confirmed individually across store text, patch notes and hero descriptions:</p>
<p><strong>Stun · Knockdown · Bleed · Burn (Fire) · Shock (Electrocution) · Freeze (Ice) · Poison · Airborne · Break</strong></p>
<p>Break is the odd one out — it is super-armor break rather than a damage-over-time or crowd-control state, and it is itself a Signal trigger. Castella's kit is built on it.</p>
<figure class="fig"><img src="/images/dragonsword-burning-field-ring-1200.webp" srcset="/images/dragonsword-burning-field-ring-800.webp 800w, /images/dragonsword-burning-field-ring-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 720px" alt="A ring of fire burning across a grassy field around a hero mid-attack, with orange embers filling the screen" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Burn is one of nine confirmed Status Ailments. Applying one arms a Signal Skill on whichever hero responds to it. Official screenshot — DragonSword: Awakening, Hound13 Inc.</figcaption></figure>
<p>We list nine because nine are individually attested. Hound13 has never published a canonical list, and Poison in particular rests on a passing player comment about team composition. If you find a site listing exact build-up thresholds and durations for each, ask where those numbers came from.</p>
<h2>How a Signal actually fires</h2>
<p>Signal Skills have <strong>no cooldown</strong>. They arm when the right ailment lands, and then one of two things happens:</p>
<ul><li><strong>The responder is on-field</strong> → you get a button prompt.</li><li><strong>The responder is off-field</strong> → an icon appears next to their portrait, and switching to them fires the Signal Skill as they tag in.</li></ul>
<figure class="fig"><img src="/images/dragonsword-party-hud-skill-effect-1200.webp" srcset="/images/dragonsword-party-hud-skill-effect-800.webp 800w, /images/dragonsword-party-hud-skill-effect-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 720px" alt="In-game HUD showing a party panel with Reina, Cerese and Dana on the left, the minimap top right and the skill bar bottom right, while a towering ice effect erupts in the centre" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>The party panel on the left is where an off-field hero's Signal Skill icon appears — switching to them fires it as they tag in. Official screenshot — DragonSword: Awakening, Hound13 Inc.</figcaption></figure>
<p>That second case is the whole design. Switching is not a defensive escape or a resource swap, it is <em>the attack</em>. The store calls it "Endless Signal Switching," and with three heroes each holding 2 Active and 2 Signal Skills, you have twelve skills in rotation.</p>
<p>The consequence, which players discover the hard way: <strong>teams want overlapping ailment focus, not complementary damage.</strong> Three heroes who each apply an ailment nobody else responds to will simply never chain, no matter how good their individual numbers are.</p>
<h2>Basic attacks are not filler</h2>
<p>Basic attacks do very little damage. They exist to <strong>chip skill cooldowns and build ailment stacks</strong>. If you are mashing your way through a fight and wondering why your skills feel unavailable, that is the mechanism working as intended — you are meant to be weaving basics between skills, not treating them as a fallback.</p>
<h2>The rest of the verb set</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Dodge / dash attack</strong> and <strong>running attack</strong></li><li><strong>Jump and air attack</strong>, gated by stamina</li><li><strong>Grab</strong> — WWE-style slams on small enemies, Monster-Hunter-style mounting on large ones</li><li><strong>Air combos</strong>, which is why Airborne is a whole build archetype</li><li><strong>Parry</strong> — universal, but <strong>Alex</strong> is built around it and can parry raid <a href="/guides/bosses/">bosses</a></li></ul>
<p>Difficulty rises automatically with your Mercenary Corps rank and can be manually lowered. Content offers selectable tiers up to <strong>Very Hard</strong>, <strong>Nightmare</strong> and <strong>Extreme</strong>.</p>
<h2>Team archetypes players actually run</h2>
<p>Six recur in community discussion: <strong>Bleed, Burn, Shock, aerial (Airborne), summon, and defensive control.</strong></p>
<p>The aerial team is the most self-contained — Astria, Eileen and Aria all launch, so the chain feeds itself. Stun is the most forgiving because Lute applies it and is also the roster's only on-demand heal.</p>
<p>There is no in-game compatibility chart. The way to build a team is to read each hero's skill text and find their trigger, which is exactly what long-time players advise.</p>
<h2>Enemies that gate on element</h2>
<p>Some enemies take <strong>no damage at all</strong> until hit with the correct element. <strong>Soulwarden Zemoor</strong> is the standard example, and the game does signpost it — there is a note on a nearby staircase stating he is weak to shock and burn. Ornette (electrocution) is the usual answer; Kalien is the community's standard pick against undead generally.</p>
<p>If a fight feels like your damage is being ignored, read the environment before you rebuild your <a href="/guides/gear-runes-and-karma/">gear</a>.</p>
<p>Boss mechanics also key off ailments. <strong>Hagen the Dark Necromancer</strong> heals, and the counter is exploiting the Break window rather than out-damaging the heal.</p>
<h2>What the game tells you, and what it does not</h2>
<p><strong>Confirmed tells:</strong> an on-screen button prompt when an on-field hero's Signal arms, a portrait icon when an off-field hero's does, visible juggling and launching on Airborne, interruptible boss animations, and damage numbers on every hit.</p>
<p><strong>Not documented:</strong> per-ailment icons, colours or sound cues. And critically, <strong>no allowed source describes any cleanse, resist stat, or immunity system.</strong> We are not going to assert one exists. If you are taking repeated ailment damage, the available answers are positioning and food, not a cleanse button.</p>
<h2>Two achievements tied to this system</h2>
<p><em>Status Ailment Master</em> is hidden, with a 10.8% global unlock rate (read 1 September 2026) — low enough to suggest it wants something more specific than "apply ailments a lot." <em>Who Cast the Frenzy?</em> is also hidden. Neither condition is public.</p>
<h2>Where this system came from, and what it costs</h2>
<p>DragonSword launched in Korea in December 2025 as a free-to-play gacha before Hound13 terminated its publishing deal and relaunched on Steam as a buy-to-play package. The tag-team system survived that transition intact, and it is the thing reviewers consistently praise.</p>
<p>What did not fully survive is the surrounding structure. Only <strong>three party slots</strong>, only <strong>two equipped consumables</strong>, and no dedicated healer besides Lute are all constraints that read as gacha-era design in a single-player game. They are worth knowing before you plan a team, because they are not going to change.</p>
<h2>A note on the English text</h2>
<p>The English localisation has real problems — one 92-hour positive review describes incorrect pronouns and a location called both "Mistflower" and "Mistblossom." Hound13 has shipped localisation fixes in 1.0.4 and 1.0.7 and lists improved localisation as ongoing work. When a skill description reads ambiguously, that is a plausible explanation, and checking the damage numbers on a training dummy is more reliable than parsing the tooltip.</p>5:["category","guides","d"]
