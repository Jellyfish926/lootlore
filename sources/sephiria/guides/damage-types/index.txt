3:T2a0f,<p>One sentence reorganises this entire system: Chaos damage "now returns 'true' for all four attack type conditions: Fire/Ice/Lightning/Physical."</p>
<p>That is why Fire bonuses appear to fire on attacks that are not Fire. Chaos is not a fifth element sitting alongside the others; it satisfies all of them at once.</p>
<p>It comes from the <strong>'Combo System' Public Beta announcement of 1 October 2025</strong>, the densest source on damage attributes TEAM HORAY has published — and one that needs a date stamp on every number taken from it.</p>

<h2>The four attributes</h2>
<p>TEAM HORAY's own text names four attack type conditions: <strong>Fire, Ice, Lightning, Physical</strong>. Those are the four flags an attack can satisfy, and they are what conditional bonuses check against.</p>
<p>There is no separate "magic" attribute anywhere in the material we checked. A guide that talks about magic damage in Sephiria is using shorthand for a spell-based weapon line, or vocabulary imported from another game.</p>
<h2>Chaos counts as all four</h2>
<p>The change is stated as a before and after:</p>
<blockquote>(Previous) Chaos Damage: A separate attribute not corresponding to "Fire, Ice, Lightning, Physical" (Changed) Chaos Damage: An attribute that corresponds to <strong>all damage types</strong> including "Fire, Ice, Lightning, Physical"</blockquote>
<p>The practical consequence: a Chaos source benefits from every elemental bonus you carry rather than none. Under the old rule, stacking Fire stats and then dealing Chaos damage was a dead end. Now Chaos is the attribute that cashes in whatever you specialised into.</p>
<p>This was not a clean switch. Patch <strong>0.9.5</strong> on 31 October 2025 shipped "Fixed an issue where the Chaos damage type was not applied properly in certain situations," a month after the rule changed — so the behaviour needed a follow-up. If you are reading a guide written before November 2025, assume it describes the old rule.</p>
<h2>Void is where "unclassified" went</h2>
<p>The same announcement continues:</p>
<blockquote>The "Unclassified" attribute concept that previously corresponded to Chaos damage has been replaced with a new attribute type called <strong>"Void Damage"</strong>, which has been applied to some elements.</blockquote>
<p>Void's definition falls out of the old Chaos definition: it corresponds to none of Fire, Ice, Lightning or Physical. Chaos took the "counts as everything" role; Void inherited the "counts as nothing" role Chaos used to hold.</p>
<p>One effect is documented crossing that line. Patch <strong>0.9.3</strong>, 24 October 2025: "Amethyst Summon attack attribute changed. (Chaos damage -&gt; Void damage)." TEAM HORAY re-sorts effects between the two buckets, so an attribute you memorised in one patch can move in the next.</p>
<p>TEAM HORAY has not published a list of which effects are Void. "Applied to some elements" is the whole of it.</p>
<h2>Execute stopped being a flat 400%</h2>
<p>Also from the same announcement:</p>
<blockquote>Previous: Deals 400% of damage. (Does not count as a critical hit) Changed: Critical hit damage multiplier is doubled. (Counts as a critical hit)</blockquote>
<p>Two things changed: Execute became a function of your own critical damage, and it stopped being excluded from critical-hit status.</p>
<p>That reverses who Execute is good for. A flat 400% is worth the same to every build; a doubled critical damage multiplier is worth more the more critical damage you already stacked, and little to a build that never invested. What it resolves to in absolute numbers is not published, so there is no break-even point to give.</p>
<h2>Enemies gain Defense as stages progress</h2>
<blockquote>As stages progress, enemies are now granted "Defense". (Instead, HP has been adjusted down to match the damage reduction rate.)</blockquote>
<p>This is the structural change behind late-floor difficulty: enemies were not given bigger health bars, they were given a damage reduction stat with health pulled back to compensate. The 1.0 update added a related line under Combat Rules — "Travelers in the dungeon now grow stronger as stage progress advances."</p>
<p>The arithmetic is missing. TEAM HORAY has never published how a point of Defense converts into damage reduction, so nobody can say what a Defense value is worth. Our <a href="/bosses/chapters-and-bosses/">bosses page</a> carries a base Defense figure per stage, but that column comes from a community compendium, not from TEAM HORAY. <a href="/guides/hard-mode/">Hard Mode</a> adds modifiers that push enemy stats further still.</p>
<h2>True Damage and Ignore Defense: one sentence in the entire record</h2>
<p>These are the two stats people ask about most and the two with the least documentation. In the whole published patch history, TEAM HORAY names them once, in <strong>0.10.10</strong> on 23 January 2026:</p>
<blockquote>The player's True Damage and Ignore Defense stats now also apply to Companion.</blockquote>
<p>That is it. Both stats exist and both propagate to your Companion. No formula, no scaling, and no statement of how they interact with the Defense system above.</p>
<p>There is one community account — a Steam guide describing each of the four attributes as having a base value of 20 modified by attack method, and stating that "regardless of the type, all damage types are added to the true damage level." It is a <strong>single source, not independently confirmed</strong>, last updated 19 May 2025 against version 0.7.35. We record that it exists rather than adopt its numbers: reprinting Early Access arithmetic as a 1.0 formula is how bad numbers spread.</p>
<h2>Electrocute, and the date on the number</h2>
<blockquote>Electrocute — A debuff that deals <strong>180% of lightning attribute damage after 2 seconds</strong>. (Replaces the previous "Erosion" debuff) Stacks up to 2 times, and stacking does not refresh the duration.</blockquote>
<p>The last clause is the one that changes play: a second stack does not extend the timer.</p>
<p>Every figure in that quote was last publicly adjusted in the October 2025 beta announcement. TEAM HORAY has not restated the 180%, the two-second delay or the two-stack cap for 1.0. Treat them as the most recent published values, not as confirmed current ones.</p>
<h2>Trance is the one number 1.0 restated</h2>
<p>Trance appears in the 1.0 weapon upgrade text at two tiers, both trading away the same thing:</p>
<ul><li>"You can no longer use Special Attack: Parry, but Weapon Attack has a <strong>20% chance</strong> to grant Trance. (Trance can be stacked up to <strong>2 times</strong>)"</li><li>"You can no longer use Special Attack: Parry, but Weapon Attack has a <strong>30% chance</strong> to grant Trance. (Trance can be stacked up to <strong>4 times</strong>)"</li></ul>
<p>Because those lines are in the 1.0 announcement of 31 July 2026, they are current in a way most numbers on this page are not. Patch 0.9.6 carried 20% with a cap of 2, so the higher tier is a genuine 1.0-era increase on both the chance and the ceiling.</p>
<p>What Trance actually grants is not documented. The patch notes describe how you obtain it and how high it stacks, and stop there.</p>
<h2>Which of these numbers is actually current</h2>
<p>The announcement that defines Chaos, Void, Execute, enemy Defense and Electrocute describes itself as provisional:</p>
<blockquote>we decided to first gather various data through a public beta, refine the system, and then transition it to an official update in the future</blockquote>
<p>Its own Fruit Skewer section adds that parts "may be modified or removed depending on future development progress." So the correct way to read every figure taken from it is <strong>"last publicly adjusted in the October 2025 beta post"</strong>, not "the 1.0 value." The mechanics — Chaos satisfying all four conditions, Void being unclassified, Execute keying off critical damage, Defense growing by stage — have not been contradicted since, and 0.9.3 and 0.9.5 behave consistently with them. The percentages have never been reconfirmed.</p>
<p>Only two things on this page carry a 1.0-dated source: the Trance values and the Travelers scaling line.</p>
<h2>Checked against 1.0.30</h2>
<p>Three weapon-side changes since this page was written: <strong>Breath of Ice</strong> (Sword &amp; Shield) now really grants +40% Dash Attack Damage — 1.0.22 fixed it silently giving 30%; <strong>Lightning Flash</strong>'s Special Attack <em>Issen</em> grants invincibility while sheathing (1.0.26); and <strong>Frozen Bow</strong> arrows pass through walls (1.0.30). Damage-type definitions and the stat list are unchanged.</p>
<h2>What we will not give you</h2>
<p>No damage formula. TEAM HORAY has not published how attributes, True Damage, Ignore Defense and enemy Defense combine into a final number, and a wrong reconstruction is worse than none.</p>
<p>No element ranking. There is no first-party tier list and no community survey behind one — the same refusal our <a href="/builds/build-routes/">build routes page</a> makes, and that page also notes nobody has published a damage benchmark for any route.</p>
<p>No enemy weakness chart: nothing we checked describes per-enemy elemental resistances. And no per-item attribute data — placement and level mechanics are on the <a href="/guides/inventory-grid/">inventory grid page</a>, but which artifacts carry which attribute is not in the public record.</p>
