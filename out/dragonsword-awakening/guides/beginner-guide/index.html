    DragonSword Beginner Guide: Your First Hours

    By Jellyfi · Last verified 2026-09-01

    Game version checked: 1.0.10 (19 August 2026 patch). DragonSword patches frequently — if a newer version is live, cross-check tuning-sensitive numbers in-game.

    First, a correction that will save you an hour of confusion: DragonSword: Awakening is not a roguelite. It is a single persistent open world — the Continent of Orbis — with a chaptered main story. There are no runs, no procedural stages, and nothing resets. If you arrived expecting a run-based game, that expectation will fight you.

    What it is: an anime-style open-world action RPG in Unreal Engine 5, built around tag-team combat driven by status ailments, with 8 chapters and a main story the producer estimated at 10–15 hours (about 20 to complete all eight).

    If you prefer watching the systems in motion, GameBreakerGod's long-form video guide covers the same ground from 150 hours of play &mdash; useful as a second opinion next to the written route below.

    The one system to learn first

    You field three heroes and control one at a time. Each has 2 Active Skills and 2 Signal Skills — twelve skills in play across the team.

    The loop: apply a status ailment → a Signal Skill arms → firing it switches heroes → the incoming hero applies theirs → repeat.

    Signal Skills have no cooldown; they gate on ailments instead. If the responder is off-field, an icon appears on their portrait and switching to them fires the skill as they tag in.

    Two consequences that are not obvious:

    Basic attacks are not filler. They do little damage but chip skill cooldowns and build ailment stacks. Weaving them is the intended rhythm.

    Your team needs overlapping ailments, not complementary damage. Three heroes who each apply an ailment nobody else responds to will never chain. Read each hero's skill text for their trigger — the game does not show you a compatibility chart. Our combat page covers this in detail.

    What to do about heroes early

    Eight of the nineteen heroes join through the story. The other eleven are bought in the Exchange with Fate's Invitation tickets, and there is no other source — no gacha, no paid characters. The game gives out enough invitations to eventually get everyone, so an early purchase is not a permanent loss.

    Still, spend the first few on ailment coverage rather than on whoever looks strongest. Lute is the only hero with an on-demand heal, so he stays in most teams by default; build the other two slots around a shared ailment.

    Healing, and why cooking matters immediately

    There is no natural HP regeneration. Out of combat you heal at a Goddess Statue of Organa. In combat, food is your healing, and you can only equip two consumables at a time.

    So cooking is not a side activity — it is the healing economy. Three methods: Grill restores HP or stamina, Boil gives buffs and some revival teas, Slice cuts stamina cost while climbing, swimming, dashing and flying. Recipes are discovered by experimenting, and three 4-star ingredients make a 5-star dish.

    Patch 1.0.7 helped a lot here: statue usage is now unlimited, and dungeon entry and wipe recovery restore you to 100% HP with the party revived. If you read older complaints about respawning at 20% health, those are outdated.

    What you will actually be doing

    Beyond the main story ("Adventure Quests," numbered to at least #59), the content list is long: Hero Quests, Bond Quests, Area Quests, Sudden Missions, Hunt dungeons, Trait dungeons, daily-resetting World Bosses, 3-player Raids, the Tower of Trials, Trial of Mist jumping puzzles, 29 sliding-pillar puzzles, pressure-plate puzzles, treasure maps, waypoints, and collectibles.

    Progression layers on top: hero levels, Mercenary Corps promotion quests that raise the level cap as story beats, Awakening tiers, Karma and Exclusive Karma, Master Skills, gear with substats, Runes with synthesis, and Familiar set bonuses.

    That is a lot of systems, and reviewers do call the material sprawl excessive. Do not try to engage with all of it in your first ten hours.

    Co-op, briefly

    Co-op runs on invite codes and only in specific content: Battle supports 2 players, Raid supports 3. There is no open-world co-op and no player trading. The producer said the 2-player subjugation cap was deliberate — "with 3 or more players, normal boss combat became too chaotic" — and that raids were scaled down from an original 8-player design. Co-op is host-based, so there are no regional restrictions.

    The known issues to patch around

    Three are worth knowing before you start:

    D3D12 crashes on certain GPUs and drivers. Hound13 has acknowledged this as an open issue with a workaround: add -dx11 to the game's launch options. This is the single most useful thing to know if the game will not start.

    A startup crash after certain graphics settings. The fix is deleting DS\Saved\Config\Windows\GameUserSettings.ini. Several players also report forced upscaling they could not disable without this.

    Save loss. Multiple users report saves vanishing — one lost 31 hours, another after quitting during a bugged quest. No mods were involved in either case. Back up DS\Saved\SaveGames\[SteamUserID]\ periodically. Hound13's stated policy is that mods and save editing are unsupported.

    A fourth, if you are on Linux or Steam Deck: a "file tampering" error blocking lobby creation (joining still works) has been reported across Bazzite, Fedora and openSUSE, with one user resolving it only through a full reinstall.

    What to expect from the story

    An honest note, because it affects whether you buy: the main plot is not resolved when the credits roll. Multiple reviewers describe a cliffhanger ending and at least one unfinished questline, attributing it to the game's origin as a gacha title designed for continuous updates. Hound13 has a roadmap with four free heroes and further patches, but there is no guarantee of a story conclusion on any particular schedule.

    The reception is nonetheless strongly positive — roughly 90% across 11,700+ reviews as of 1 September 2026, with praise concentrated on the combat feel, art and optimisation, and criticism concentrated on pacing, cutscene density and DLC structure.

    Fifty-nine achievements

    There are 60 Steam achievements (1.0.9 added the sixtieth), 17 of them hidden. The most-unlocked is Lucky! at 45.9% (read 1 September 2026), which gives you a sense of how early most players stop. If you are achievement-hunting, note that several tie to content types you might otherwise skip entirely — Sudden Missions, Hunt monsters, Eona's Legacy collectibles and dungeon gates each have one.
