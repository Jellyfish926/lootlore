3:T1862,<p>DragonSword: Awakening runs a prologue plus <strong>eight chapters</strong> across one persistent map, ending in a fight against <strong>Ursula at level 70</strong>. This page maps that spine — and, more usefully, the three places where players stop moving forward and wrongly conclude the game is broken.</p>

<p>A scope note up front: this is a chapter map, not a quest-by-quest script. Two walkthrough sites cover the fine detail and they are named in the sources box; what we add is the structure and the stuck-points, verified against player threads.</p>
<h2>The story spine</h2>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Chapter</th><th>Where and what</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Prologue</td><td>Orbis kingdom — Lute, Johnny and the mercenary band are introduced</td></tr><tr><td>1</td><td>Orbis — guild work and reputation building; goblin cave with a powder-keg gimmick</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td>The Organa order — a saint's death; the three factions take the stage</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td>Journey to Lord Barduk — ends, in the walkthrough's words, in tragedy</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td>Dragon Worshippers active; barrier stones and the northern fortress</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td>Barrier stones destroyed — <strong>Gauron, the Origin Dragon, invades</strong>; misty wastes, the rock tribe, rescuing Reina</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td>An abandoned temple, ancient texts, the cult mystery; the mural-door puzzle</td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td>Tablet fragments; palace preparations for the dragon subjugation; the lightning-crystal mechanic</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td>The joint operation — dragon nest, dragon eggs, tunnel network, and the ending</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>How long that takes: the developer's own line is <strong>10–15 hours for the main story</strong>, with 30–40 hours including side content. Players in the length thread report about 30 hours with light exploration; one YouTuber claims 150 hours for everything. We could not find a HowLongToBeat entry to settle it, so those player numbers are the best available calibration.</p>
<h2>The two named bosses we can confirm</h2>
<p><strong>Gauron, the Origin Dragon</strong>, is the story's engine — the chapter 5 invasion, then the target of the subjugation you spend chapters 7 and 8 preparing for.</p>
<p><strong>Ursula</strong> is the actual final fight, at <strong>level 70</strong>, with a true-dragon transformation partway through. The Whisper of the House walkthrough dedicates a page to the fight and the ending that follows.</p>
<p>Per-chapter boss rosters for chapters 1–4 and 6 exist in fragments across sources that do not agree with each other, so this page does not print a chapter-by-chapter table. What <em>can</em> be read reliably is the achievement data — sixteen boss names with their difficulty gates — and that gets its own treatment on the <a href="/guides/bosses/">boss list</a>, which complements this chapter map rather than repeating it.</p>
<h2>Roadblock one: the Ent that is not in your way</h2>
<p>The most common "I'm stuck at level 30" report traces to one encounter. Around main quest 40, players run into an <strong>Ent roughly ten levels above the story curve</strong>, get one-shot, and conclude they have hit a grind wall.</p>
<p>The player thread resolves it: <strong>the Ent is optional open-world content that happens to stand near the route. Walk around it.</strong> Actual story bosses stay level-matched. If a fight feels tuned for someone ten levels ahead of you, in this game that is the signal you are fighting the wrong thing, not that you need to farm.</p>
<p>Two legitimate levers exist if you are genuinely underpowered: gear, <a href="/guides/gear-runes-and-karma/">Karma</a>, awakening and skill upgrades let level-30 players punch far above their level, and the guild difficulty setting drops open-world enemies by ten levels without touching story bosses.</p>
<h2>Roadblock two: the chapter 3 "trap" that is scripted</h2>
<p>In the Dragon Worshipper altar section of chapter 3, a <strong>forced teleport</strong> yanks you out of the area mid-exploration. It looks exactly like a bug, and players report it as one. The walkthrough is explicit: it is a scripted story event. Nothing was lost and nothing needs re-doing — keep playing forward.</p>
<h2>Roadblock three: puzzles, not power</h2>
<p>Between the two incidents above, the walkthrough's overall verdict on this game's difficulty is worth internalising: <strong>the places players stall are navigation and puzzle sections, not combat spikes.</strong> Chapter 6's mural door — where you replicate the arrangement from murals seen earlier — and chapter 7's lightning crystals are the pattern examples. If you have been circling an area for twenty minutes, the answer is in the environment you already passed, not in your build.</p>
<p>One related melee note from the level-30 thread: melee heroes are widely reported as harder going than ranged through the mid-game. If your frontline party keeps dying to fights the story expects you to win, trying a ranged-led composition is a cheaper fix than grinding — the <a href="/heroes/tier-list/">tier list comparison</a> covers who fills those roles.</p>
<h2>After the credits</h2>
<p>Chapter 8's ending is not a save-wipe; the world stays open. What we have not verified is the exact post-game state — what respawns, what locks, and whether anything is missable before the finale. Rather than paraphrase forum guesses, we are leaving that section empty until we can check it against our own finished save; the sources box will grow when it happens.</p>5:["category","guides","d"]
