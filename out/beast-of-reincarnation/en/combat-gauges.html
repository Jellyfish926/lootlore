    FP, Down Gauge and Entanglement: The Three Combat Gauges

    Combat Gauges (FP, Down Gauge, Entanglement Gauge)

    Quick facts

    Three meters, one verb: a clean parry pays into all three simultaneously. FP is Koo's shared resource pool; the Down Gauge belongs to the enemy and is a stagger/execution meter; the Entanglement Gauge is Emma's own and charges her Entanglement Overdrive burst.

    Combat · Systems

Put together, the striking thing is that a single clean parry pays into all three economies simultaneously — here is what each bar is for, which Spirit Stones feed which, why two different systems both slow time, and the one fight where a whole gauge disappears.

    Three bars, one verb

    Beast of Reincarnation asks you to track three meters at once, and our coverage explains each of them on a different page: FP on the Koo guide, the Down Gauge on the boss page, the Entanglement Gauge on the builds guide. Put together on one page, the striking thing is that all three are filled by the same action.

    Parry. A clean deflection fills FP, contributes to the Down Gauge, and — with the right Spirit Stone — feeds the Entanglement Gauge as well. Our builds guide's phrasing for the design is that defense literally feeds offense; what the three-bar view adds is that a single successful block is paying into three separate economies simultaneously. That is why every page on this site ends up giving the same advice from a different angle, and why our Koo guide's diagnosis for a fight going badly is almost always “parry more, dodge less”.

    FP: the bar that belongs to the dog

    FP is short for Florescence Points, and our glossary defines it as the resource Emma banks from clean parries and Koo spends through his command menu on Bloom Arts. Three properties of it are worth holding on to.

    It is a single pool, not two. Our Koo guide is explicit that there is no separate “dog mana” to manage — your parry game and Koo's output are the same economy. That collapses two resource-tracking problems into one.

    It banks between exchanges. Our boss page describes the rhythm as parry to earn, bank until an opening, then spend — FP carries across a boss's aggressive phase so you can cash it in when the boss commits to a long recovery. It is a savings account, not a regenerating stamina bar.

    It is written two ways. Our walkthrough notes that the acronym appears as both Focus Points and Florescence Points depending on the source, and our builds guide carries the same caveat. This site standardises on Florescence Points; if you meet “Focus Points” elsewhere, it is the same bar.

    What you cannot learn from this site is what anything costs. Our Koo guide states the reason directly: FP costs are tuning values that shift with patches, so it points at the in-game menus instead of freezing a number. No Bloom Art on this site carries a price.

    The Down Gauge: the bar that belongs to the enemy

    The Down Gauge is the odd one out, because it is not yours. Our glossary defines it as a stun meter that fills as you land hits and clean parries on an enemy; break it and a boss is opened to heavy punishment, or a lesser Malefact can be executed outright. Every enemy carries one alongside its health bar.

    Our Koo guide draws the tactical conclusion that follows and it is the most actionable single sentence in our combat coverage: Koo is your stagger engine, so the highest-value moment to spend FP is when an enemy's Down Gauge is nearly full. Firing a Bloom Art then converts the gauge break into an execution or a boss stun; firing it on cooldown converts it into a few ordinary hits. Same FP, very different return.

    The Down Gauge is also what makes one whole build direction work. Our builds guide describes stealth executions as leaning on the Down Gauge to thin packs of lesser corrupted enemies before they aggro — and flags the limit honestly, that sealed-boss fights still open as straight confrontations, so stealth is an exploration and clearing layer rather than a boss strategy.

    The Entanglement Gauge: the bar that belongs to Emma

    The third meter is Emma's own, and it is the one with hard numbers attached. Across our builds guide, weapons page and boss page, the recorded facts are consistent:

    • It builds across seven segments or layers.
    • Filling it lets Emma trigger Entanglement Overdrive, a slow-time burst window.
    • Overdrive upgrades across five levels.
    • It is unlocked by investing in Strength — the same attribute that raises attack and defense.

    That last point is the one people miss. Our builds guide lists Strength as raising attack and defense and unlocking Overdrive, which makes it the only attribute doing double duty: it is your survivability-and-damage stat and the key to the ultimate. Our weapons page draws the same conclusion from the weapon side. If you want Overdrive online early, Strength is not optional.

    Our boss page calls Overdrive one of the safest ways to open a stubborn boss, and it is the only one of the three gauges that keeps working when Koo is not with you.

    Two slow-time systems, easily confused

    Both Koo's command menu and Emma's Overdrive slow time, and our Koo guide names mixing them up as a common early mistake. They are not related.

    WhoseKoo'sEmma's
    Opened by△ on PS5, on demand, any timeSpending a full Entanglement Gauge
    CostsNothing to open; the art you pick costs FPThe whole gauge
    What it is forA tactical window to read the fight and issue a commandA charged offensive burst

    The menu's slow-down is the more important of the two habits, because it is free. Our Koo guide's advice is to open it when you are confused, not only when you are spending: a second-phase transition, a swarm spawn or an unfamiliar wind-up all become readable. One crucial caveat it repeats — the menu slows time but does not pause it. Emma still has to move, space and defend in real time while you read the board.

    Stones that feed the bars

    Seven entries on our Spirit Stones guide exist to top these bars up, and grouping them by which bar they feed is a faster way to pick than reading the effect list straight through:

    Entangled ParryParryingChance of Entanglement Gauge +0.2 and Koo's FP +1; rate scales 10% to 50% across five levels
    Entangled DodgeDodgingSame values and the same 10%–50% scaling

    Entanglement IncreaseExploiting an elemental weaknessBoth bars; rate scales 3% to 7% across five levels
    SharpshooterExploiting a weakness with a ranged weapon1 FP to Koo; recovery rate 20% to 30% across three levels
    Entanglement StoneStart of combatOpens the fight with both bars already raised; Lv1–3 gives +1/+1 to +3/+3

    Two observations the stone list makes available. First, Entangled Dodge exists, which means dodging is not resource-dead if you pay a stone slot for it — a useful hedge for a build that cannot hold a parry rhythm. Second, the two weakness-triggered stones are worth nothing against a boss with no weakness, which our elemental weakness guide shows is exactly how the final boss is recorded. Stone value is not constant across the campaign.

    The fight with one bar missing

    Chapter 9's Crimson Malefact is the campaign's stress test of everything on this page, because it removes one of the three bars entirely. Emma fights it alone: no Koo, no Bloom Arts, and therefore no reason to bank FP at all. What is left, in our own coverage's words, is katana pressure, dodge timing, parry discipline and the Entanglement Overdrive gauge Emma builds independently of Koo.

    The practical reading is that Overdrive is the one gauge you can never be separated from, so the Strength investment that unlocks it is the most fight-proof point spend in the game. Our Koo guide adds a habit warning worth repeating: if your instinct under pressure is to open the command menu, retrain it before Chapter 9 rather than during it. There is nothing there.

    A spending order that holds all game

    Drawing the three pages' advice into one sequence:

    1. Parry rather than dodge whenever the attack allows it — one action pays into all three economies, and our Koo guide notes the best players fish for parries deliberately.
    2. Bank FP through the aggressive phase instead of spending defensively. Our boss page warns that burning FP in a panic leaves nothing when the real opening arrives.
    3. Watch the enemy's Down Gauge, not your own bars. It is the clock that decides when your FP is worth the most.
    4. Spend the Bloom Art as the Down Gauge approaches full, converting a stagger into an execution or a stun.
    5. Hold Overdrive for the wall. It is the charged option and the one that still exists when Koo does not.

    What this page does not claim

    No number on this site describes the size of any bar. We have no FP cap, no FP gain per parry, no Down Gauge values, no duration for Overdrive at any of its five levels, and no figure for how much a single hit contributes to a stagger. The stone percentages above are the only hard numbers in our gauge coverage, and our Spirit Stones guide itself advises verifying them in-game since balance patches can adjust them. We also cannot tell you what “Entangled” — the achievement at 56.9% on the 31 August read and 57.1% on the 7 September read — actually requires, because Steam publishes no description for it.

    Gauges FAQ

    What does FP stand for?

    Florescence Points, though some sources write it as Focus Points. Our walkthrough flags the inconsistency and defers to the in-game text; this site uses Florescence Points throughout.

    How do I fill FP fastest?

    Perfect blocks and parries. Landing ordinary attacks contributes, but our Koo guide names precise defense as the engine, and says a dodge-only style starves the second half of your kit.

    How many segments does the Entanglement Gauge have?

    Seven, per our builds guide and weapons page, and Overdrive itself upgrades across five levels.

    Does the command menu pause the game?

    No. It slows time to a crawl without pausing — Emma still moves and defends in real time while the menu is open.

    Can I fill the Entanglement Gauge without parrying?

    Yes, partly. Our weapons page records that landing hits as well as deflections fills it, and the Entangled Dodge stone gives dodges a chance to contribute. Parrying remains the most efficient route.

    Which gauge should a new player focus on?

    The enemy's. Our Koo guide's framing is that Koo is a tempo tool rather than bonus damage, and reading the Down Gauge is what turns FP from a number you accumulate into damage you actually collect. The beginner guide covers the parry fundamentals underneath all of it.
