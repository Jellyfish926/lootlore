Nothing in the store data points at a phone or tablet build.</p>

Across all twelve official Steam announcements — from the playtest in May 2026 through the update on 5 August 2026 — the words <em>mobile</em>, <em>Android</em>, <em>iOS</em>, <em>phone</em> and <em>tablet</em> do not appear once. Mumpitz Games has not teased a port, given a release window, or answered the question publicly.</p>

<h2>Why so many people assume it is a phone game</h2>
<p>This is not a silly question, and the game's own store data explains why it gets asked so much. Steam lists it with a <strong>Mouse Only Option</strong>, <strong>Playable without Timed Input</strong>, and <strong>Save Anytime</strong>.</p>
<p>That is the exact feature profile of a mobile title. Nothing here demands a keyboard, precise timing, or an uninterrupted session — you click to place turrets, click to fire abilities, and stop whenever you want. A tower defence with an incremental-style upgrade tree is also the genre people most associate with phones.</p>
<p>So the instinct is reasonable. The build just does not exist.</p>4:Tced,<h2>What the APK and "free download" sites are</h2>
<p>Search suggestions for this game include <em>mobile download</em> and <em>mobile free download</em>, which means those pages exist and are getting traffic. They are not from Mumpitz Games.</p>
<p>The game is a paid PC title at <strong>$9.99</strong> and it is sold through Steam. There is no official free build, no official APK, and no legitimate mirror. Anything offering an Android package of this game is either a mislabelled different game, a repackaged file, or worse — and installing an APK from an unknown source is a considerably worse idea than waiting.</p>
<p>We are not linking to any of them. If a mobile version is ever announced, it will show up in the Steam announcement feed and on the developer's own channels first.</p>
<h2>The handheld answer that does exist</h2>
<p>If what you actually want is to play this lying on a couch rather than specifically on a phone, there is a real option: <strong>the game has a native Linux build, and its stated minimum Linux OS is SteamOS 3.8 or similar.</strong> That is Steam Deck's operating system.</p>
<p>Valve's compatibility report puts it at <strong>Playable</strong> — the middle rating, not Verified. The report is specific about why, and both reasons are interface problems rather than performance ones:</p>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Test</th><th>Result</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Default configuration is performant</td><td>Pass</td></tr><tr><td>Default controller config fully functional</td><td>Pass</td></tr><tr><td>Controller glyphs match the Deck</td><td><strong>Fail</strong></td></tr><tr><td>Interface text is legible</td><td><strong>Fail</strong></td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>In plain terms: it runs fine and the controls work, but the on-screen button prompts show the wrong icons and the UI text is small enough that Valve flagged it. For a game where you are reading turret stats and upgrade costs, that second one is the real cost.</p>
<p>Our <a href="/platform/pc-and-steam-deck/">PC and Steam Deck page</a> has the full requirements if you want to check a specific machine.</p>
<h2>Would it work on a phone if it were ported?</h2>
<p>Probably, and that is the frustrating part. The install is 200 MB, it needs 4 GB of RAM, and there is no multiplayer component to maintain. The heaviest requirement is Vulkan 1.3 support, which modern phone hardware generally has.</p>
<p>None of that means a port is coming. Mumpitz Games is a small self-publishing studio, and the game sold 100,000 copies in its first four days on PC — a studio in that position usually spends the next few months on updates for the platform that is already working.</p>
<p>We will update this page if that changes. We are not going to guess at a date.</p>
