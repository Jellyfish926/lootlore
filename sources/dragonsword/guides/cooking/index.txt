3:T1cbc,<p>There is <strong>no natural HP regeneration</strong> in DragonSword: Awakening. Out of combat you heal at a Goddess Statue of Organa. In combat, only <strong>Lute</strong> has an on-demand heal — and you can carry just <strong>two equipped consumables at a time</strong>.</p>
<p>That is the context that makes cooking a core system rather than a pleasant diversion. Food is how you heal during a fight, and there is no substitute.</p>
<h2>The three methods</h2>
<div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>What it produces</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Grill / Roast</strong></td><td>HP or Stamina restoration</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Boil</strong></td><td>Buffs — attack up, defense up. Some boil recipes yield <strong>teas that act as revival items</strong></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Slice</strong></td><td>Reduces stamina consumption while flying, swimming, dashing, climbing and clambering</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>The Slice category is easy to dismiss and should not be. Traversal in Orbis is stamina-gated across five separate movement types, and the jump-puzzle content (Trial of Mist) is where players report the most friction. A stamina dish is the difference between clearing a climb and falling off it.</p>
<p>Boil's revival teas are the more important discovery for combat. With one healer in the roster and two consumable slots, a revival item in one of those slots changes what a wipe costs.</p>
<h2>How recipes work</h2>
<p><strong>You do not buy or find recipes. You discover them by experimenting.</strong> Combine ingredients, and if the combination is valid, the dish is added to your cookbook permanently.</p>
<p>There are <strong>73 recipes</strong>, or 74 if you count the "Mysterious Dish." A single dish usually has <strong>several valid ingredient combinations</strong>, which is what makes experimentation viable rather than a lottery — you are not hunting one exact triple.</p>
<p>A <strong>Cookbook filter</strong> was added for the full release specifically in response to demo feedback, so if you played the June demo and found the list unmanageable, that has been addressed.</p>
<h2>Ingredient quality maps directly to dish quality</h2>
<p>The star rating of your ingredients drives the star rating of the dish. The rule players have worked out: <strong>three 4-star ingredients yield a 5-star dish.</strong></p>
<p>That is a straightforward optimisation once you know it. Do not cook with whatever is at the top of your bag — sort by star rating first, and save your high-star ingredients for the buff dishes you actually want at maximum strength.</p>
<h2>Ingredients that are not used in anything</h2>
<p>Worth knowing so you stop hoarding them: <strong>pistachios, cheese, sugar, carrot with white flowers, pepper, tofu, polished rice, almonds, butter and salt</strong> are not required by any recipe.</p>
<p>They still occupy inventory. If you are short on space before a long expedition, those are the safe things to leave behind.</p>
<h2>Where cooking happens</h2>
<p>You cook from the field and from the menu, and every hero has their own cooking animation and cinematic — Hound13 has patched them individually, which tells you how much bespoke work went in. <a href="/guides/patch-notes-and-roadmap/">Patch 1.0.7</a> fixed <strong>Reina's</strong> weapon clipping through her arm during the <strong>[Boil]</strong> and <strong>[Slice]</strong> actions, and a separate bug where characters showed a default pose during cooking cinematics. Adventure Quest 59 had a bug where <strong>Johnny's</strong> cooking icon displayed as Lute's.</p>
<h2>Bugs fixed, and one thing to check</h2>
<p><strong>Radish Soup was always crafted at 1-star quality</strong> regardless of ingredients, until patch <strong>1.0.7</strong>. If you tried it before that and wrote it off, try again.</p>
<p>More importantly: a launch-day patch <strong>reset some players' discovered recipes</strong> — specifically players who had been on a pre-launch build. Cooking recipes, mini-game records, titles, <a href="/guides/gear-runes-and-karma/">Karma</a> Codex entries and Sudden Mission discovery were all affected. If your cookbook looks emptier than you remember, that is the likely cause and the recipes are re-discoverable.</p>
<h2>How cooking fits the healing economy after 1.0.7</h2>
<p>Patch <strong>1.0.7</strong> materially changed the surrounding systems, and it is worth understanding what it did and did not fix:</p>
<p><strong>Fixed:</strong> the Goddess Statue of Organa usage limit was <strong>removed entirely</strong> — statues are now unlimited. Dungeon entry and wipe recovery now start you at <strong>100% HP with all fallen characters revived</strong>, replacing the previous respawn at low health.</p>
<p><strong>Not fixed:</strong> there is still <strong>no [Retry]</strong>. Hound13 added <strong>[Quick Entry]</strong> for <a href="/guides/co-op/">multiplayer</a> content and explicitly stated that <strong>[Retry] was delayed to the following week.</strong> The complaint that generated this — a 59-hour review describing four consecutive loading screens after a failure — was about the retry loop specifically.</p>
<p>So cooking's role has shifted slightly. Before 1.0.7, food was compensating for a hostile recovery system. Now it is doing the job it was designed for: in-fight healing and pre-fight buffs.</p>
<h2>A practical routine</h2>
<ol><li><strong>Gather while traversing.</strong> Ingredients are scattered across the continent and you will walk past most of them. There is no separate farming loop.</li><li><strong>Sort by star rating before cooking.</strong> Three 4-star ingredients is the target.</li><li><strong>Fill one consumable slot with a heal, one with a buff or revival tea.</strong> Two slots is the hard limit; decide deliberately rather than by whatever was cooked last.</li><li><strong>Keep a Slice dish for traversal content.</strong> Trial of Mist and the sliding-pillar puzzles are where stamina actually runs out.</li><li><strong>Experiment with leftovers.</strong> Discovery is permanent and there is no penalty for a failed combination beyond the ingredients.</li></ol>

<h2>What is not published</h2>
<p>The <strong>full recipe table</strong> — which ingredients make which dish — does not exist in any source we consider reliable. It circulates on auto-generated wikis and in one YouTube video; we have not transcribed it because we cannot verify it, and a wrong ingredient list wastes materials that take real time to gather.</p>
