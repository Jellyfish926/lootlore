He falls in with Johnny, a mercenary, and Castella, an Elf, and is pulled into mercenary life as a shadow spreads over the continent. It is an open-world action RPG, not a roguelite — one persistent map, eight story chapters, nothing resets.

Combat is the part most guides get wrong. You field three Heroes and control one at a time, and each carries two Active Skills and two Signal Skills. Signal Skills have no cooldown; they arm when a specific **Status Ailment** lands on the target. That single rule is what makes team building a question of overlapping ailment coverage rather than raw damage — three Heroes who each apply an ailment nobody else responds to will never chain, no matter how good their individual numbers look.

Two systems surprise people in the first hour. There is **no natural HP regeneration**: out of combat you heal at a Goddess Statue of Organa, in combat only Lute has an on-demand heal, and you can carry just two equipped consumables. That is why Cooking is a progression system here and not a side activity. And some enemies take no damage at all until struck with the right element — if your damage feels ignored, read the environment before you rebuild your gear.

The Steam release is buy-to-play with **no gacha and no paid characters**. Eight Heroes join through the story; the other eleven are bought in the Exchange with Fate's Invitation tickets the game hands out as you play. Four more — Ryza, Jerome, Veronica and Logan — are on the roadmap, and Hound13 has stated they are part of the full version rather than paid DLC.
